# Entertainment-first Commercial Strategy — WORKING_CONCEPT

## Core thesis
The Exchange is fictional IP owned by an independent entertainment company, not a disguised marketing funnel for an AI filmmaking course. **Entertainment attracts attention; universe creates attachment; IP opens diversified revenue.** Protect emotional canon from sponsorship direction.

## Revenue categories
1. Sponsorship / brand integrations: character-compatible clothing, beauty, accessories, hospitality or meaningful physical props; clearly disclosed. Do not invent actual deals or audience metrics.
2. Platform revenue: eligibility- and policy-dependent, supplemental rather than guaranteed.
3. Direct fan experience: working name **The Registry** for optional member experiences—bonus scenes, aftershows, archive experiences, production notes, optional digital collectibles. Main canon intelligible without paywall.
4. In-world commerce: Relay-inspired design objects, ledger notebooks, jewelry/fashion/fragrance partnerships, limited licensed collections (design concepts, not commitments).
5. B2B creative studio: commissioned narrative advertising and brand mini-films once production reliability proven, separate from compromising Sloane's storyline.
6. Education/licensing/tools: tested creative methodology, consistency workflows and classes only AFTER production credibility. Do not mirror another creator's course or claim unverified buyer counts.
7. Longer-term licensing / formats / spin-offs: only when audience traction and commercial rights are evidenced.

## Product placement boundaries
Brand enhances but never dictates family tragedy, mother's final exchange or a forced character decision. Protect rights and AI performer representations. Paid endorsements disclosed. Avoid promoting financial pressure on grieving people as glamorous. Distinguish fictional brands/devices from real paid partners.

## Unit economics and operational planning
Track sponsored revenue, platform income, memberships, merchandising margin, professional services and license fees separately. Fully loaded costs include inference credits, rejected generations, editing, voice/music, labor, legal/IP and rework. Hypothetical illustrative numbers from conversation ($2,500 sponsor package, $8 monthly membership, etc.) are NOT market prices or revenue estimates. Establish baseline cost per part and conversion before pricing.

## Commercial rollout
1. Creative/IP proof + rights/production reliability.
2. Audience/retention data + sponsor-compatible inventory + selective brand outreach.
3. Optional fans/community + low-inventory world merch tests.
4. Services + education built on validated demonstrable expertise.
5. Licensing/spin-off expansion.

## Rights/compliance to research
Commercial image/video model terms; voice and likeness rights; original music/sound rights; brand/IP clearances; advertising disclosures; audience privacy and minors; use of AI content and distribution platform policies; contracts, trademarks, brand-safety clauses. Requires current legal/platform research before public launch; no legal determination in this bible.

## Commercial principle
Audience must never sense that Sloane exists to sell them things; the things exist because audiences love Sloane and her world.
