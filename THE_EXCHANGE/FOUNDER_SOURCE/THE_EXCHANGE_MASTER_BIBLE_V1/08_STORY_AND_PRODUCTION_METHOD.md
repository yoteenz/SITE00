# Story Development and Production Methodology

## Primary format — founder-selected
Ten complete initial exchange stories, released as **three parts each**, yielding **30 primary releases**. Episodic title/slate still subject to review. Each part must provide entertainment and a meaningful development; do not merely cut scripts into equal thirds. One complete exchange resolves by end of Part 3, with some linked consequences crossing over. Not every episode needs a clue or special device.

## Part structure — default, not formula
- **P1 / Request:** lively hook, desire, stakes, arrangement invitation, reversal.
- **P2 / Cost:** human complication, value mismatch, moral choice, escalation, earned reason to return.
- **P3 / Resolution:** significant human payoff, consequences, optional personal beat/callback—not an endless cliffhanger.
Alternative openings include an old Recall, a misunderstanding, unexpected collaboration or ordinary social situation.

## First-episode audience contract
Open in a joyful, colorful, socially uninhibited scene where recording restrictions are naturally visible but not lectured. Reveal playful Sloane first. Encounter creates a curious, self-contained arrangement. Avoid family-tragedy dump, exposing mother's death details, full tech catalog, explanation of the Quiet Network and immediate romance announcement. **Current party opener is an attractive proposed scene, not a locked screenplay.**

## Progressive disclosure
Early: show joy, people, Sloane, single novel norm/device. Mid: deepen her professional capabilities, relationships, family tensions and rare tech. Later: satisfy earlier questions and introduce a small earned family link. Don't conflate *unknown to viewer* with *unknown to writer*.

## Quality review questions
- Would a newcomer watch without studying the wiki?
- Does each part give payoff beyond teaser?
- Does someone make a credible human choice?
- Does Sloane's personality create plot rather than merely react?
- Is technology governed by clear limits?
- Is this episode distinct from prior cases?
- Is emotional subtext organic and not repetitive?
- Does humor survive alongside mystery?
- Do flashbacks change meaning rather than simply inform?
- Is the resolution ethically credible and consent-compliant?
- Does sponsored integration, if any, preserve dramatic independence?

## Creative workflow
Source bible → Opus independent critique (recommendations only) → founder adjudication → season/episode treatment → part-by-part beat sheets → scripts → continuity & reveal checks → visual authority → storyboard/shotlist → keyframes → verified video generation → continuity & audio review → edited episodes → delivery/marketing derivatives. Existing NDXBOOK engineering is a possible shared utility and remains fictionally separate.

Do not publish Part 1 before Parts 2 and 3 are completed/quality checked. Suggested cadence 3 parts/week is an experiment, not locked calendar. Track watch-through, P1→P2→P3 conversion, episode completion, rewatch and retention, discussion quality, costs per finished minute, and sponsor effects.
