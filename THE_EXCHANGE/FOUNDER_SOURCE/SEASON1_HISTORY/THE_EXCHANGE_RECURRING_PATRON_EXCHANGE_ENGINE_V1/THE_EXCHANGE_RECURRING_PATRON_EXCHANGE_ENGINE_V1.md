## Season 1 development package: requests, lives, nights and the season arc

### What this package does

The completed hybrid has a strong season story: Sloane's last Grand Exchange as the one who decides, the Living Legacy, the two estranged collaborators, the apparent breach, and Vivienne's withdrawal. What it underuses is the thing the institution is for: **people approaching The Exchange with an extraordinary wish and something meaningful to offer.** In the hybrid's 74 releases, almost every request belongs either to the Living Legacy or to the season mystery.

This package restores that engine without consuming the rest:

- **Ten selected exchange scenarios** (PX-01 to PX-10), chosen from twenty candidates, spread across all seven arcs. They vary in scale and genre: a big band and a rival club, a stranger on a bus, two radio hosts who've never met, a superstar's night off, an orange seller's debt, a church-hall spades table, a sprinter's dawn race, fourteen hundred hats, a proposal that fails, and a coat Sloane refuses.
- **A complete revised release map** of 86 releases (S1-01 to S1-86), with every one of the 74 baseline releases accounted for and a fictional-day column for wardrobe continuity.
- **A proposed answer to the step-back question.** The hybrid has Sloane step back from deciding. Read literally, that removes the activity the show needs her to anchor. Six interpretations are set out for the founder; one is recommended and none is canonized.

## 02 — The Recurring Exchange Engine

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.**

### 02.1 The engine in one paragraph

Someone wants something they can't get by asking, buying or pushing. They have something real to give. Someone else, who owes them nothing, has to say yes. Sloane sees the connection nobody else sees, finds the right way in, and lets the people decide. Sometimes it works beautifully. Sometimes it turns into something better than what was asked for. Sometimes it fails, and when it fails, part of the failure is hers.

That's the whole machine. It doesn't need forms, hearings or dashboards. A viewer who has seen nothing else should understand the wish and the offering in the first twenty seconds.

### 02.3 How a request travels, as the audience sees it

1. **The door.** A referral card on the desk, a brass token on a fruit scale, a friend confessing across a vintage rail. Who sponsors a patron is part of the fun: a rival, an objector, a former patron.
2. **The wish and the offering, in one breath.** Spoken aloud, early, plainly.
3. **Sloane's read.** What she notices that nobody else would: a hand-rolled lapel, a laugh she's heard on the radio, a spade on a mantel. Sometimes she reads it wrong (PX-07, PX-09).
4. **The person who can say no.** Named on screen. The Exchange can arrange a meeting; it can't make anyone agree.
5. **The place where it happens.** A night, a dawn, a table, a bus, a dance floor.
6. **The ledger line.** Settled, modified, failed or declined. Heard once, all together, at the end of the season (S1-83).

### 02.9 The step-back question

**What the founder asked for (Sprint 4 §5A, founder-selected):** "Sloane is contemplating stepping back from personally facilitating The Exchange. She is not necessarily retiring from life or permanently abandoning the institution." The deeper reason was left open.

**What the hybrid built (hybrid proposal):**
- Her motive is the weight of her word: her opinion has become a verdict.
- She tells Gideon this Grand Exchange is her last "as the one who decides" (LL-09).
- She pauses personal sponsorships (LL-11). The makers inherit the choosing (LL-15).
- On stage, she calls it "her last Grand Exchange as their facilitator, not their host" (LL-63). She walks past the makers' window and doesn't go in (LL-71).

**Does it undermine the premise? Yes, as currently worded.** The hybrid bundles two different things:

- **Deciding:** choosing the Living Legacy; vouching people into the network; her word as a verdict on someone's life.
- **Facilitating:** the craft of making an impossible request happen.

The hybrid's own motive argues for giving up the first. LL-63's wording gives up the second. If Season 1 ends with Sloane no longer facilitating, any later season has a protagonist who hosts parties while other people do the work. That is the "passive executive" the sprint rules out. It would also repeat a beat: in the hybrid's backstory she already stepped back once (the Quiet Years, Y−9 to Y−5).

**The motive is strong. The wording is the problem.** Measured against the baseline, stress-test question 19 is a FAIL (§14).

### 03.3 The ten candidates not selected

**C-11 Category Is.** A ballroom house wants to hold its thirtieth-anniversary ball inside the city's Museum of Decorative Arts, after hours, among the historical gowns.
- *Offering:* the house's archive of hand-made ball costumes, for the museum's collection.
- *Who decides:* the chief conservator, who has refused because the gowns are fragile.
- *Complication:* she walked balls herself decades ago and has never told her board.
- *Turn:* she walks one category, in white gloves, between the vitrines.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Spectacular and culturally rich, but VERY HIGH to produce, and it competes with the Grand Exchange for spectacle in the season's last third.

**C-12 Twenty-Two Birthdays.** Augustine Pratt, a florist of seventy, has left flowers on one woman's doorstep, anonymously, on her birthday for twenty-one years. He asks to meet her on the twenty-second and tell her who he is.
- *Offering:* the shop itself, to run as she likes for a week.
- *Who decides:* the woman.
- *Turn:* she has known for years, and has been leaving the empty vase on his shop step.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Lovely, but the season already carries three romances (Sloane and Emmett, Tad and Yaz, Marcus and Renée).

**C-13 The Lobby.** A chief executive wants to learn double dutch again from the girls' team that practises outside her tower; she was a champion at twelve.
- *Offering:* her lobby, for practice on rainy days.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Charming but slight, and its shape (an older woman, a younger athlete) repeats Lane Four.

**C-14 The Coat-Check Key.** A stranger arrives through no channel at all, holding a brass Halcyon House coat-check key from thirty years ago, and asks to collect what was checked. This is the bible's **Breach** route.
- *Decision:* **Reserve, as a hook only.** Explaining it would open a new mystery and risk merging it with Vivienne's. The nameless patron at the door in S1-83 gestures at the Breach without explaining anything.

**C-15 Jollof Court.** Two aunties, one Nigerian and one Ghanaian, have argued about jollof at every wedding for thirty years. They want a blind tasting judged by a chef who refuses to judge anything.
- *Offering:* both recipes, written down for the first time.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Food is already carried by Ezekiel's supper (LV-1) and the cookout (PX-04), and the rivalry shape overlaps Off Air.

**C-16 The Glass Piano.** A glassblower wants to build a playable glass piano for a pianist who is losing his hearing.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Beautiful, but it is a maker creating a work of love for someone. That is the Living Legacy's format, which the sprint says requests must not repeat.

**C-17 The Last Run.** A grandson wants one last run of the decommissioned night train for his grandfather's ninetieth birthday.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** It overlaps the railroad history in Ezekiel's supper, and it is nostalgia-led.

**C-18 The Unreachable House.** A woman wants one hour inside her childhood home. The new owners won't answer.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** A lost-family-legacy story, the type the sprint warns against repeating.

**C-19 The Smallest Patron.** Jalen, eleven, Nadine's son, approaches The Exchange properly, with a referral from his grandfather. He wants his mother to take one whole day off.
- *Offering:* his savings ($41) and a month of dishes.
- *Who decides:* Nadine.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Delightful, but Nadine already carries the spades thread and her sabbatical (S1-85). The bible also asks for extra care with minors.

**C-20 The Revenge Invitation.** A socialite wants a Grand Exchange invitation for one reason: her rival has one.
- *Offering:* a great deal of money.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Money alone can't buy an invitation (founder-supported invitation culture). It's usable as a ten-second gag in any office scene; it is not added to the map.

### The inventions in use

**Pledge token** (PX-05)
- *What it is:* a brass token stamped with the year a promise was made, linked to a verifiable record of the terms.
- *What it allows:* a promise made decades ago can be produced, read and recognized by both parties.
- *First experience:* S1-12. Mavis, the fruit seller, sets a brass token worn smooth and stamped 1986 on her scale. Sloane takes it like a jewel.
- *Why it's appealing:* it is jewellery and a receipt at once. Mavis walking into the Crescent Room holding it high (S1-20) is the shot.
- *Relation to the request:* it proves Richard owes her a dance. It cannot make him dance.
- *Consent limit:* it records an agreement but can't compel fulfilment or prove sincerity (Bible 06). Richard says yes because he wants to.
- *Feasibility and cost:* one practical prop. No VFX. Low.

**Relay paper** (PX-02, and Celestine's note in S1-86)
- *What it is:* paper that stays blank to everyone except the reader it was written for.
- *What it allows:* a private, authenticated note that can be handed across a crowded bus.
- *First experience:* S1-15. Sloane has Tina write her letter on it, and the page goes blank. The payoff is S1-34: the card travels hand to hand around a sewing circle, unreadable, until Tina's writing blooms in Ophelia's hand.
- *Why it's appealing:* handwriting appearing in someone's hand is intimate and simple.
- *Relation to the request:* it gets Tina's invitation to Ophelia without exposing her. It doesn't make Ophelia say yes.
- *Consent limit:* it proves who sent the note, not that the note is true or kind (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* an ink-reveal insert, practical or a simple composite. Low. It resolves the bible's "Relay" naming question by making the paper the physical object and the Relay the protocol.

**Constellation** (PX-06, shown by its limit)
- *What it is:* the bible's permission-based map of introductions and obligations.
- *What it allows on screen:* nothing, which is the point. "No registry can tell Sloane who plays spades." A night nurse can (S1-18).
- *Why it's appealing:* a world where people still know things the system doesn't.
- *Consent limit:* it doesn't reveal undocumented links (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* none. It is a line of dialogue.

**Echo Track** (PX-07)
- *What it is:* footage of a past performance laid over the real place where it happened, as a moving line of light.
- *What it allows:* a person can race, walk or dance beside a recorded version of themselves.
- *First experience:* S1-40, at dawn. Lane four lights up with a pale line that runs Dee's 1985 record stride for stride, drawn from archive footage of her own race. "That's you?" "That was me."
- *Why it's appealing:* light on a track at dawn, and a record you can see.
- *Relation to the request:* it gives the race a third runner. It doesn't decide who wins.
- *Consent limit:* it plays only what was recorded, with the athlete's consent. It can't recreate an unrecorded moment (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* one light-streak VFX element over a real location. Medium. The technique is reusable.

**Interval silk** (PX-09)
- *What it is:* a hanging silk panel that holds a stored, consented message and plays it on a cue.
- *What it allows:* someone who can't be present can be part of a moment, asynchronously.
- *First experience:* S1-47. Abena's recorded message is set to bloom the moment Renée says yes. Renée doesn't. On the silk, Abena waits mid-smile.
- *Why it's appealing:* the most romantic object in the season becomes its saddest image.
- *Relation to the request:* it brings the best friend to the proposal. It can't change the answer.
- *Consent limit:* stored, consented content only. It is not live and cannot respond (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* one held frame projected on silk. Low to medium.

**The Hush** (PX-03)
- *What it is:* a sound field only the people inside its circle can hear, with smaller private pockets within it.
- *What it allows:* a rooftop party with music everywhere for the guests and silence for the neighbours. Two people in a pocket can talk privately in a crowd.
- *First experience:* S1-50. Silence, then music, as a guest steps over the line. The street below hears nothing.
- *Why it's appealing:* it can be shot as pure sound design and it creates a new kind of party.
- *Relation to the request:* it is how two rival radio hosts end up talking without knowing who the other is. Inside it they recognize each other's voices.
- *Consent limit:* it can't record and can't be heard from outside. Everyone inside chooses to step in.
- *Feasibility and cost:* sound design plus actor reactions. A faint ring of light at the edge is optional. Low to medium.

**The Halo** (PX-04)
- *What it is:* a floating hood-dryer ring that hums like a salon on a Saturday.
- *What it allows:* a set or a wig styled in minutes, in anyone's closet.
- *First experience:* S1-62. Delphine's disguise is set under it in Sloane's closet.
- *Why it's appealing:* it puts the salon at the centre of the season's technology instead of a lab.
- *Relation to the request:* it helps the disguise. The disguise works because of Sloane's craft and Gideon's lesson in walking like someone with nowhere to be.
- *Consent limit:* it styles hair. It doesn't change a face or an identity.
- *Feasibility and cost:* one hero prop with a hover rig or simple float VFX. Medium. It could recur in Sloane's own routine; that's optional and not in the map.

**The Ember** and **the Threshold** stay exactly as the hybrid proposed. The Ember is the Living Legacy's object (copper *no*, gold *yes*; S1-01, S1-26, S1-58, S1-82). The Threshold is Kai's one night of access, with Honora's consent (S1-61, S1-63). This sprint doesn't change them.

### Held back

- **The Veil** (bible): consensual identity shielding. It would make PX-04 trivial. A famous woman who could switch her face off wouldn't need a family's discretion. Hold it for a request where shielding creates trouble instead of removing it.
- **Glint** (new idea, held): a small light on a lapel that tells any camera in the room whether the wearer may be recorded. It would turn devices-down (PX-01) from a custom into a technology. Hold it.

### Before any of these is approved

Bible 06 requires a full dossier per device: purpose, mechanism and speculative assumption, inputs and outputs, limits, permissions, failure modes, price and access class, cultural effects, novelty, effect on earlier episodes, and the tension it introduces. This section is a short-form proposal, not that dossier. None of these inventions has been tested visually. That is a production risk (§11).

### A wardrobe vocabulary for Sloane (original, for discussion)

- **She mixes eras.** Vintage pieces with sharp tailoring, never head-to-toe anything.
- **She commissions people.** Wendell's suits, Ophelia's lining. Her best clothes have a maker she knows by name, which mirrors her job.
- **She rewears.** The orange suit comes back. Her last-season jeans go to Delphine. Rewearing is character, and it eases continuity.
- **Colour.** The world's palette is amber (Bible 02), and the Ember is copper. Sloane's signature should sit against those, not inside them; orange is a statement she earns at S1-28, not her default.
- **Movement test.** Every hero look must survive a dance floor. This is a production constraint as much as a character note.
- **Hair is part of the wardrobe.** The Halo (S1-62) puts salon culture at the centre of the season's technology. Hair changes should follow fictional days, not releases.
- **Black creative culture in the clothes.** Church hats, a sewing circle's finishing techniques, wax-print linings, a Ferry Street tailor. These are the season's fashion sources, not luxury brands.

### The reference accounts

The founder named five Instagram accounts as direction: @chinchillyonstyle, @alyciabella, @ishateria, @dearra and @kaylarwill.

- **They were not reviewed.** This environment couldn't view them. Nothing in this section claims to describe them.
- **They are direction only.** No look is reproduced, no person is depicted, no partnership is proposed, and no images were made.
- **What they should become.** When the founder or a costume designer reviews them, the useful output is principles (silhouette, colour, proportion, hair, accessories, how the person moves), not specific outfits. Those principles should then be checked against the vocabulary above.
- **No brands.** The package names no real fashion brands. Wendell Ames, Lorraine Vandyne, Tina's magazine and Ophelia Grant are fictional and need name clearance.

Two days carry most of the risk. ## 10 — Four-Layer Coverage Report

> **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Counts come from the layer tags in the revised map (§09) and are recomputed by script, not hand-entered.

### The four layers

- **R · Requests.** A patron asks for something, offers something, and someone with the power to say yes decides.
- **L · Lives.** The city and the people in it, outside the plot: markets, salons, radio, church, the river track.
- **N · Nights.** Events The Exchange itself hosts: the opening party, Exchange Nights, the Grand Exchange. Other people's parties (the Crescent Room, the Salon) count as L, not N.
- **A · Season arc.** The Living Legacy, the leak, the step-back, the family.

A release can carry several layers. The tags are deliberately strict, so the gaps below are real.

### What works

- **Requests are steady.** Every arc has three to five request releases (4 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 4 / 4 / 3). Only 3 of the 30 request releases are unchanged baseline rows (S1-05, S1-52, S1-61); the other 27 are new or revised. Requests now run through the whole season instead of living mainly in the Living Legacy's nominations.
- **The longest stretch without a request is six releases** (S1-55 to S1-60). It is deliberate: those releases carry the Living Legacy's central revelation (Richard's account of the Ember at S1-58), and a request there would compete with it.
- **Requests meet the arc without explaining it.** PX-01's payoff is the stage for Royce's toast (S1-28). PX-07 comes through Honora's referral the day after her objection (S1-37). PX-09 comes through Royce's sponsorship of Marcus (S1-45). None of them is caused by the season mystery, and the mystery doesn't solve any of them.
- **Lives are strongest in the first half** (Arcs 1–3: 26 of 40 releases carry L), which is when the audience needs to fall for the city.
- **The Grand Exchange gathers everything.** Six scenarios pay off inside Arc 6, and every one is closed in Ada's ledger (S1-83).

### Weaknesses, stated plainly

1. **Nights vanish for most of the season.** After the opening party (S1-01 to S1-05), no Exchange-hosted night appears until the rooftop at S1-50, a gap of 44 releases. Arcs 2 and 3 have none. Other people's rooms (the Crescent Room at S1-20, the Salon at S1-28) carry nightlife in the meantime, but the founder's direction is that The Exchange's own nights should be legendary. This is the package's biggest coverage gap. It is put to the founder as a decision (§12) rather than fixed by adding releases without approval.
2. **No release carries all four layers.** S1-28 and S1-50 come closest. That is acceptable for social-first releases, which work best with one or two jobs, but it means no single release demonstrates the whole show.
3. **Arc 5 is arc-heavy.** Twelve of fourteen releases carry A and only four carry L. The city goes quiet while the family story peaks.
4. **Afterlight has no Night.** The season ends on private moments. That may be the right ending, but it should be a choice.
5. **Some territories are still thin.** Travel, food as craft, architecture and visual art have little or no request coverage (see the variety matrix in §02.6). The unselected candidates in §03 cover several of them.

- **Only two faces are approved canon:** Sloane and Gideon. Every other face, including the six recurring patrons, needs a locked identity before production.
- **Hard cases:**

  - Young Dee: the Echo Track can avoid a face entirely by showing only a line of light. That is cheaper and safer.
  - Ensemble scenes (S1-24, S1-64, S1-72) with several recurring faces in one frame are the most likely to break identity consistency.
- **Mitigations:** a reference sheet per recurring patron; group scenes tested early; the Interval silk shown as one held frame instead of moving video.

### Feasibility verdict

The additions are mostly LOW and MED releases in reused sets, and four scenarios need no effect at all. The real risks are identity consistency for a larger recurring cast and the untested look of the Echo Track and the Halo. Those should be tested before the founder commits to the 86-release version.

## 13 — Final Editorial Recommendation

> **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** This is an editorial opinion for the founder. It does not change canon or the approval matrix.

### The short answer

**With the recurring exchange engine restored, Season 1 reads as a complete and renewable series rather than a single mystery with a party at the end.** The season still builds to the Grand Exchange and the Living Legacy, but between the opening and the finale, people now walk into The Exchange wanting things, offering things, and getting answers the audience can enjoy on their own. That is what a series needs to come back.

It is complete on paper. It is not yet proven on screen.

### Why it now works as a series

- **The engine runs all season.** Every arc has three to five request releases, and the longest gap is a deliberate six releases during the Living Legacy's central revelation (§10).
- **Requests are their own pleasure.** A woman who wants to lose at cards, two radio rivals, a fruit seller collecting a forty-year-old dance. None needs the season mystery to matter (§02, §04).
- **Sloane works.** She reads people, makes calls, takes risks and gets things wrong (§02.8). Her fashion is part of how she thinks (§07).
- **The premise survives the farewell.** Under Alternative A she gives up the verdict, not the craft. The last thing the season shows is a new patron at her door (S1-83).
- **There is supply.** Six recurring patrons, ten developed reserve candidates, and whole territories of the city still untouched (§03, §06).

### Serious weaknesses

1. **The Exchange's own nights disappear for 44 releases** (S1-06 to S1-49). The founder's direction calls for legendary parties; the middle of the season doesn't deliver them. This is the most important fix (decision S-3).
2. **Everything visual is untested.** The Echo Track, the Halo, Delphine's disguise and the ensemble scenes are all identity or effects risks for AI video. Only Sloane's and Gideon's faces are locked (§11).
3. **The step-back is still the founder's call.** If the hybrid's wording stays, the finale ends the engine. This package implements Alternative A, but it is not approved (decision S-1).
4. **The cast grows.** Eleven new on-camera faces and fifteen new names to clear. Each is a casting, identity and continuity cost.
5. **The season is longer.** 86 releases and 125:50 against the baseline's 74 and 100:55. A leaner version of about 81 exists, at the cost of the time between requests and payoffs (decision S-2).
6. **Too many happy endings.** Seven of ten requests succeed in some form. PX-09 and PX-10 carry the failures. If a scenario is cut, it shouldn't be one of those two.
7. **City life may start to look arranged.** The market and the Crescent Room now host requests; the audience could begin to read every city moment as a setup (stress question 10).
8. **Arc 5 goes quiet on the city.** Only four of its fourteen releases carry city life while the family story peaks.
9. **Source gaps.** The Lantern draft wasn't available, the founder's V2 document isn't in the repository, and the five style references weren't reviewed. Nothing in this package depends on them, but each could change a recommendation.

### What I would do next, in order

1. Decide S-1. Alternative A, with E's openness kept as a dial, is the recommendation.
2. Decide the hybrid's spine (FD-01). The ten scenarios mostly survive any spine, but two hooks depend on the hybrid.
3. Decide S-3 (the Nights gap) and S-2 (86 or about 81) together.
4. Run a small proof-of-concept slate before locking any scenario: the opening (S1-01 to S1-05), *Spades* (S1-24), *Dawn* with the Echo Track (S1-40) and *The Yes* (S1-77).
5. Lock identities for the six recurring patrons and clear the new names.
6. Then approve scenarios one at a time.

### What this package does not do

It doesn't start Season 2 or rewrite the fictional universe. It doesn't change the Series Bible, the approval matrix, canon, GitHub, Supabase records, production systems or brand relationships. It generated no images and no wardrobe. Every original element is **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED** and is returned for the founder's review.

### Evidence

**1. Is THE EXCHANGE itself fascinating to watch? PASS.** Its rules are shown in action rather than explained. A gift that arrives with a request is still a fee, so Sloane refuses the coat (S1-19). A Recall can deliver a promise but can't enforce it (S1-12, S1-20). Referral cards arrive in surprising hands (Honora's, S1-37; Clarence's and Bunny's, S1-33). The year closes with a ledger read aloud (S1-83). *Risk:* the institution's mechanics are dense; each release shows one rule at most.

**2. Does Sloane actually facilitate memorable requests? PASS.** She identifies a sewing circle from a lapel (S1-15), recruits the hospital's best card player at 3 a.m. (S1-18), throws a rooftop party for two people who don't know they're both invited (S1-50), disguises the most famous voice in the country in her own closet (S1-62), and turns a market into a river of hats (S1-51).

**3. Can a new viewer understand what each patron wants? PASS.** Each request is stated in the opening moments in one or two lines: "Tell your father he owes me a dance" (S1-12); "I want to meet her" (S1-15); to lose, fairly, once (S1-18); "One ordinary night. Nobody knowing" (S1-62). PX-07 deliberately misdirects (mentoring, then racing, S1-37), but the stated request is still clear.

**4. Is the offering concrete and meaningful? PASS.** A spade carved in 1974 and a month of Sunday dinners (S1-18). Every dish washed (S1-62). A start never explained in an interview (S1-37). A phones-down hour for a phones-up club (S1-09). Fourteen hundred hats (S1-51). Six months of weekends on sponsored cases (PX-09, S1-45). The weakest is PX-02's magazine cover, and the story knows it: Ophelia turns it down (S1-34).

**5. Is each exchange emotionally or visually distinct? PASS.** No two share a territory (§02.6): a club (S1-28), a bus (S1-34), two radio booths (S1-33), a backyard (S1-64), a dance floor (S1-20), a church card table (S1-24), a river track (S1-40), a street market (S1-51), a balcony (S1-47), a sofa (S1-19). Registers run from romantic comedy (PX-03) to quiet heartbreak (PX-09) to a declined bargain (PX-10).

**6. Is the protagonist active rather than reactive? PASS.** She pauses her sponsorships (S1-12), chooses to help a rival (S1-09), declines the coat (S1-19), misreads Dee and fixes it (S1-37, S1-40), and announces her own change (S1-75). Her mistakes are hers: she never asks Renée what she wants (S1-47).

**7. Do requests generate satisfying outcomes? PASS.** Seven succeed or succeed in a changed form, one fails (PX-09) and one is declined (PX-10). Every one is closed in Ada's ledger (S1-83). Outcomes have costs: PX-01's success hands Royce the stage for his toast (S1-28).

**8. Does the season avoid becoming a repetitive procedural? PASS.** The shapes vary: one release (PX-10 at S1-19), two releases (PX-02, -03, -04, -06, -07; for example S1-18 and S1-24), or folded into existing releases (PX-05 at S1-12 and S1-20; PX-08 at S1-51). Openings vary: a fruit stall (S1-12), a shop (S1-15), a door (S1-03), a mother walking in (S1-18), two radio shows (S1-33), a referral card (S1-37), a truck (S1-51), a garment bag (S1-19). Four scenarios use no invention. *Risk:* five new freeze-frames could make the openings feel formulaic (decision S-4).

**9. Is the Living Legacy clearly distinct? PASS.** Living Legacy visions are nominated by someone who loves the holder, made privately and unveiled at the Grand Exchange. Requests come from the patron and land wherever they happen (§02, table). The visions pay off at the Grand Exchange (S1-73, S1-74, S1-77); the requests pay off on a track, in a backyard, in a laundromat (S1-40, S1-64, S1-81). No request is caused or explained by the season mystery. PX-04 and PX-09 share people with the arc (Delphine once sang at Halcyon, S1-19; Marcus is in the leak chain, S1-45) but none of its secrets.

**10. Do Life Moments still feel spontaneous? PARTIAL.** Thirteen of the baseline's fifteen Life releases are unchanged (for example S1-21, S1-42, S1-53, S1-84). But the city's signature public places now host arranged requests (the market at S1-12 and S1-51, the Crescent Room at S1-20), and the two new Life-category releases are arranged by The Exchange (S1-24, S1-64). An audience may start to suspect every city moment is a setup. *Fix:* keep at least one unarranged Life release per arc in the market or the Crescent Room.

**11. Do Exchange Nights remain joyous and legendary? PARTIAL.** The opening (S1-01 to S1-05), the rooftop (S1-50) and the Grand Exchange (S1-68 to S1-80) are joyous. But no Exchange-hosted night appears between S1-06 and S1-49, and Arcs 2, 3 and 7 have none (§10). Other people's rooms carry the nightlife meanwhile (S1-20, S1-28). *Fix:* decision S-3.

**12. Does fashion feel natural to Sloane? PARTIAL.** In the writing, yes: the dance test (S1-02), the lapel clue (S1-15), the coat she refuses (S1-19), the orange suit as an apology (S1-06, S1-28), the closet as a workshop (S1-62). But the founder's five reference accounts were not reviewed, and no wardrobe has been designed or tested. On paper, it is part of her character; on screen, it is unproven.

**13. Are inventions intuitive and entertaining? PARTIAL.** Each appears as an image before it is explained (§05), and each has a clear limit: Relay paper blooming in one reader's hand (S1-34), a line of light in lane four (S1-40), silence becoming music (S1-50), Abena waiting mid-smile on the silk (S1-47). None has been visually tested, and none has the full Bible 06 dossier yet.

**14. Is Black cultural imagination meaningfully present? PASS.** It shapes the stories and the technology, not just the backgrounds: church hats as an inheritance (S1-51), the Tuesday spades table (S1-18, S1-24), hand-dancing at the Crescent Room (S1-20), slow-jam and house radio (S1-33), a sewing circle's wax-print linings (S1-34, S1-72), an archive of Black designers (S1-19), a family cookout that keeps a secret (S1-64), and the Halo, which puts salon culture at the centre of the season's inventions (S1-62).

**15. Are recurring guests memorable? PARTIAL.** Six recurring patrons have their own lives and return for their own reasons (§06): Mavis (S1-12, S1-20, S1-51, S1-69, S1-72), Tina (S1-06, S1-15, S1-34, S1-62, S1-72), Lorna (S1-18, S1-24, S1-72, S1-85), Dee and Zuri (S1-37, S1-40, S1-69, S1-74), Tad and Yaz (S1-33, S1-50, S1-74, S1-81), and Delphine (S1-62, S1-64, S1-72). But the smaller recurring guests (Ophelia, Grace, Wendell) have one or two scenes each, and none of the new faces has been cast or tested for identity consistency (§11).

**16. Are ethics meaningful without dominating every story? PASS.** Consent works as plot, not as hearings: devices-down is announced and capture after it is by consent (S1-28); Ophelia refuses the cover and keeps her name out of print (S1-34); Sunset fails because nobody asked Renée (S1-47); Sloane refuses a gift that is really a fee (S1-19). PX-06 and PX-08 have no ethical problem at all.

**17. Does the season arc remain causally coherent? PASS.** The validator confirms chronology, weekdays, ages and the leak chain (Appendix A). The requests touch the arc in three places without changing its causes: PX-01 provides the stage for Royce's toast (S1-28), PX-07 arrives through Honora's referral after her objection (S1-37), and PX-09 arrives through Royce's sponsorship of Marcus (S1-45). *Dependency:* the hybrid's breach chain (FD-08).

**18. Does the Grand Exchange retain its impact? PASS.** Its emotional centre is unchanged (Ezekiel's supper at S1-73, Noelle's song at S1-74, the *yes* at S1-77). The patrons' additions are light: keys (S1-69), arrivals (S1-72) and brief cutaways (S1-74). *Risk:* the cutaways could crowd Noelle's song; they are optional (decision S-5).

**19. Does Sloane's proposed departure threaten the long-term exchange premise? PASS as written, conditional on S-1.** Under Alternative A she stops choosing the Living Legacy and stops being the only way in, but keeps taking impossible requests: "I'm not going anywhere. If you want something impossible, you still know where to find me. Just ask me properly" (S1-75). The season ends on a new patron: "Show her in" (S1-83). If the founder keeps the hybrid's wording (Alternative F), this becomes a FAIL.

**20. Can the series generate fresh requests across multiple seasons? PASS.** The engine has renewable sources: referrals (S1-33, S1-37), Recalls (S1-12), the sponsors' circle (Tina's first sponsorship, S1-62), returning patrons (a standing Tuesday game, S1-24; Fridays at dawn, S1-40), and a city with untouched territories (travel, food as craft, architecture, visual art). The season's last request beat is a new patron at the door (S1-83). Ten developed but unselected candidates (C-11 to C-20, §03) show the supply without developing Season 2.
