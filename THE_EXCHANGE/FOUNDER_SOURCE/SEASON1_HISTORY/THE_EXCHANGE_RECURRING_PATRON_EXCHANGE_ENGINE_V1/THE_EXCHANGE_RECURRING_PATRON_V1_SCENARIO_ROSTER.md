# THE EXCHANGE — Candidates, Selected Roster and Inventions
## Recurring Patron-Exchange Engine V1

> **NONCANONICAL. PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** A development proposal for THE EXCHANGE. It changes no canon, no approval record, no Series Bible, no repository, no database record and no production system.

## 03 — Twenty Candidate Scenarios and Their Evaluation

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Twenty candidates were developed. Ten are selected and become PX-01 to PX-10 (full entries in §04). The other ten are given below in enough detail to reconsider any of them.

**Scoring:** 1–5 on each of five criteria: originality (O), emotional appeal (E), character potential (C), visual appeal (V) and narrative fit (F). Scores guided the choice but didn't decide it. Production cost, overlap with the Living Legacy, and the spread of genres across the season counted as well.

### 03.2 The ten selected candidates, in brief

- **C-01 One Private Hour.** Royce, stopped at Sloane's door on the first night, comes back through the proper one and asks for the big band that won't play his club. He offers a devices-down hour. He has to dance. → PX-01
- **C-02 The Woman on the Number 9.** A fashion editor wants to meet the best-dressed stranger on her bus. Sloane finds her through a stitch. The stranger refuses the cover and asks for a closet swap. → PX-02
- **C-03 Off Air.** Two radio rivals who've never seen each other each ask, privately, to meet the other. Sloane can't tell either one, so she throws a party. → PX-03
- **C-04 A Night Off.** The most famous voice in the country wants one ordinary family dinner, unrecognized. She'll do every dish. → PX-04
- **C-05 One Dance, Owed.** An orange seller calls in the dance Richard promised her the night Nadine was born. → PX-05
- **C-06 The Tuesday Table.** Gideon's mother wants to lose at spades, fairly, once. She wins. → PX-06
- **C-07 Lane Four.** A retired sprinter asks to coach the girl chasing her record. What she really wants is a race. → PX-07
- **C-08 Fourteen Hundred Hats.** On her eightieth birthday, an orange seller wants every one of her church hats worn on the same Saturday. → PX-08
- **C-09 Sunset.** A young man arranges a perfect proposal. It fails. → PX-09
- **C-10 The Coat.** A collector offers Sloane the coat she has wanted for fifteen years, in exchange for access to someone who isn't hers to give. → PX-10

### 03.3 The ten candidates not selected

**C-11 Category Is.** A ballroom house wants to hold its thirtieth-anniversary ball inside the city's Museum of Decorative Arts, after hours, among the historical gowns.
- *Offering:* the house's archive of hand-made ball costumes, for the museum's collection.
- *Who decides:* the chief conservator, who has refused because the gowns are fragile.
- *Complication:* she walked balls herself decades ago and has never told her board.
- *Turn:* she walks one category, in white gloves, between the vitrines.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Spectacular and culturally rich, but VERY HIGH to produce, and it competes with the Grand Exchange for spectacle in the season's last third.

**C-12 Twenty-Two Birthdays.** Augustine Pratt, a florist of seventy, has left flowers on one woman's doorstep, anonymously, on her birthday for twenty-one years. He asks to meet her on the twenty-second and tell her who he is.
- *Offering:* the shop itself, to run as she likes for a week.
- *Who decides:* the woman.
- *Turn:* she has known for years, and has been leaving the empty vase on his shop step.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Lovely, but the season already carries three romances (Sloane and Emmett, Tad and Yaz, Marcus and Renée).

**C-13 The Lobby.** A chief executive wants to learn double dutch again from the girls' team that practises outside her tower; she was a champion at twelve.
- *Offering:* her lobby, for practice on rainy days.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Charming but slight, and its shape (an older woman, a younger athlete) repeats Lane Four.

**C-14 The Coat-Check Key.** A stranger arrives through no channel at all, holding a brass Halcyon House coat-check key from thirty years ago, and asks to collect what was checked. This is the bible's **Breach** route.
- *Decision:* **Reserve, as a hook only.** Explaining it would open a new mystery and risk merging it with Vivienne's. The nameless patron at the door in S1-83 gestures at the Breach without explaining anything.

**C-15 Jollof Court.** Two aunties, one Nigerian and one Ghanaian, have argued about jollof at every wedding for thirty years. They want a blind tasting judged by a chef who refuses to judge anything.
- *Offering:* both recipes, written down for the first time.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Food is already carried by Ezekiel's supper (LV-1) and the cookout (PX-04), and the rivalry shape overlaps Off Air.

**C-16 The Glass Piano.** A glassblower wants to build a playable glass piano for a pianist who is losing his hearing.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Beautiful, but it is a maker creating a work of love for someone. That is the Living Legacy's format, which the sprint says requests must not repeat.

**C-17 The Last Run.** A grandson wants one last run of the decommissioned night train for his grandfather's ninetieth birthday.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** It overlaps the railroad history in Ezekiel's supper, and it is nostalgia-led.

**C-18 The Unreachable House.** A woman wants one hour inside her childhood home. The new owners won't answer.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** A lost-family-legacy story, the type the sprint warns against repeating.

**C-19 The Smallest Patron.** Jalen, eleven, Nadine's son, approaches The Exchange properly, with a referral from his grandfather. He wants his mother to take one whole day off.
- *Offering:* his savings ($41) and a month of dishes.
- *Who decides:* Nadine.
- *Decision:* **Reserve.** Delightful, but Nadine already carries the spades thread and her sabbatical (S1-85). The bible also asks for extra care with minors.

**C-20 The Revenge Invitation.** A socialite wants a Grand Exchange invitation for one reason: her rival has one.
- *Offering:* a great deal of money.
- *Decision:* **Not selected.** Money alone can't buy an invitation (founder-supported invitation culture). It's usable as a ten-second gag in any office scene; it is not added to the map.

## 04 — The Selected Season 1 Roster

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Ten scenarios, each with the twenty fields the sprint requires. Release references use the revised IDs. The full release text is in §09.

### PX-01 · One Private Hour

1. **ID and title:** PX-01 · One Private Hour
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Royce Bell, 46, owner of the Salon. Sponsored by Mr. Daniels, the jazz-club owner Royce once worked for as a bar-back.
3. **Desire:** the Crescent Room Orchestra on the Salon's stage for its tenth anniversary.
4. **Offering:** the Salon's first devices-down hour in ten years, and the band keeps every right to its music. Clarence adds a condition: Royce dances the first number.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Clarence Moody, 74, and the orchestra, together.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** Clarence has refused Royce four times. The orchestra plays only rooms where phones stay down, and the Salon is the city's phones-up club. Money doesn't move him.
7. **Complication:** the patron is Sloane's rival, the man she had stopped at her door. She has to facilitate for someone who wants her room.
8. **Instant interest:** a rival asking for help; a big band in a neon club; a man who doesn't dance being told he must.
9. **Interesting people:** Royce is charming and competitive, with a defensible philosophy (openness) and a bar-back past. Clarence is dignified, funny and immovable.
10. **What Sloane does:** takes the case and enjoys it far too much. She doesn't sell Royce to Clarence; she sells the hour: two hundred young people hearing a big band with nothing in their hands. She accepts Clarence's condition on Royce's behalf and finds him a teacher.
11. **Consent and agency:** the band chooses. Devices-down is venue policy, announced, and guests can step out. Royce agrees to dance. Capture after the hour is by consent.
12. **Surprising turn:** Royce dances, and for eight bars he's genuinely good. Then he uses the night she gave him to toast her "last Grand Exchange."
13. **Outcome:** succeeds, then turns against Sloane.
14. **Payoff:** the joy of a phones-down big-band hour in a phones-up club; Royce dancing; then the sting of the toast.
15. **Lingering consequence:** the toast makes her secret public. Royce owes her, and later sponsors Marcus "as an apology for the toast" (S1-45).
16. **Recurring guests:** Clarence (already recurring through Noelle's song); Royce (season principal); Mr. Daniels (voice only).
17. **Invention / visual:** none. Devices-down is a custom. The image is two hundred phones going into pockets at once.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-03 (stopped at the door, GE−92; one added line), S1-09 *Through the Door* (GE−88, 1:45, new), S1-28 *The Salon* (GE−63, extended to 2:30). Closed in the ledger at S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** S1-09 MED; S1-28 VERY HIGH (already VERY HIGH in the baseline).
20. **Season arc:** strong. The request's payoff becomes the stage for the breach. Royce's sponsorship of Marcus feeds Sunset, and with it the irony that Sloane facilitates the leak's source without knowing.

### PX-02 · The Woman on the Number 9

1. **ID and title:** PX-02 · The Woman on the Number 9
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Tina Grandison, 45, editor of the fictional magazine *Seamline*, Sloane's friend and sparring partner on shoulders. She needs no sponsor; she's already in Sloane's circle. She later becomes a sponsor herself (PX-04).
3. **Desire:** to meet the woman on her morning Number 9 bus, who for two years has dressed better than anything in Tina's pages, and to put her on the cover.
4. **Offering:** the cover.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Ophelia Grant, 61, a transit dispatcher and self-taught seamstress.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** in this world you don't photograph a stranger or follow her home. Tina doesn't know her name, and the Constellation can't find someone who never agreed to be found.
7. **Complication:** the offering is something the recipient doesn't want. Ophelia likes being nobody's discovery.
8. **Instant interest:** every commuter knows a stranger like this. A powerful woman who has never been refused anything turns shy about one person.
9. **Interesting people:** Tina is confident, funny and privately insecure that her taste can be bought. Ophelia is unflappable and exact, and she has been watching Tina too.
10. **What Sloane does:** she recognizes the hand-rolled lapels and the wax-print cuff linings as a finish taught by one church sewing circle off Ferry Street. She doesn't search for the woman. Instead she has Tina write a letter on Relay paper and sends it into the circle, where it can be read only by the person it's for.
11. **Consent and agency:** Ophelia is never identified to Tina or Sloane without her consent. The Relay paper means nobody in the circle has to reveal her. She chooses to sit across from Tina, and her counteroffer stands, including her name never appearing in print.
12. **Surprising turn:** Ophelia has been watching Tina for just as long ("I thought you'd never notice"), and she turns the cover down.
13. **Outcome:** changes. No cover; instead a one-week closet swap, and her name is never printed.
14. **Payoff:** two style-obsessed women meeting as equals; Tina refused for the first time in her life, and delighted.
15. **Lingering consequence:** Ophelia hand-finishes the lining of Sloane's Grand Exchange suit (S1-72: "A friend made it. She doesn't want her name in print."). Tina sponsors Delphine (PX-04). Ophelia receives a key (S1-69).
16. **Recurring guests:** Tina (patron, then sponsor); Ophelia (recurring guest); Inez Carter, the gown's holder, glimpsed with a life outside the Living Legacy.
17. **Invention / visual:** Relay paper, refined from the bible's Relay: blank to everyone except the intended reader. The image is Tina's handwriting blooming in Ophelia's hand.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-06 (Tina named, GE−91), S1-15 *Hand-Rolled* (GE−75, 1:30), S1-34 *The Number 9* (GE−59 to GE−57, 1:45). Paid off in S1-69, S1-72 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** S1-15 LOW; S1-34 MED.
20. **Season arc:** light. No link to the mystery. It supplies Sloane's Grand Exchange look and puts Tina into the sponsors' circle that Alternative A depends on.

### PX-03 · Off Air

1. **ID and title:** PX-03 · Off Air
2. **Patron / sponsor:** two patrons. Tad Monroe, 52, a slow-jam host, sponsored by Clarence Moody. Yaz Adebayo, 38, a house-music host, sponsored by Bunny Okafor ("Don't tell Rich I listen to house").
3. **Desire:** each, separately and privately, asks to meet the other.
4. **Offering:** Tad offers a guest night on his show, with his chair and his audience. Yaz offers to play one of his records on her show without a single joke.
5. **Who grants or declines:** each other.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** neither has ever shown a face in public. Each request is private, so Sloane can't tell either that the other asked. Asking on air would end the act.
7. **Complication:** twelve years of on-air insults. The city loves the feud, and meeting might ruin it.
8. **Instant interest:** two voices everyone knows and faces nobody does, both secretly asking for the same thing.
9. **Interesting people:** Tad is velvet, vain and kind. Yaz is fast, rude and sentimental about records. Both are night people with lives their shows never show.
10. **What Sloane does:** keeps both confidences. She designs an Unannounced Exchange that puts them on the same rooftop, then steps back and lets them find each other.
11. **Consent and agency:** both asked to meet. Neither is told who else is invited. The hush pockets let them talk privately, and they choose how to leave (ten minutes apart, in theory).
12. **Surprising turn:** inside the Hush they recognize each other's voices while both are pretending to be somebody else.
13. **Outcome:** succeeds, and continues privately.
14. **Payoff:** a glorious face-to-face argument, then a dance.
15. **Lingering consequence:** the morning after the Grand Exchange they're on air together ("Don't get used to it.", S1-81). Both receive keys (S1-69) and dance at S1-74.
16. **Recurring guests:** both are recurring patrons with independent worlds: their stations, their listeners, their rivalry.
17. **Invention / visual:** the Hush (new): a sound field only the people inside can hear, with private pockets. The image is silence, then music, as a guest crosses the line.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-33 *Both Stations* (GE−59, 1:15, in LL-28's slot), S1-50 *The Hush* (GE−36, 2:30). Paid off in S1-69, S1-74, S1-81 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** S1-33 MED; S1-50 HIGH.
20. **Season arc:** shares a night with Gideon being cleared (S1-49). No mystery link. Strengthens the Nights layer.

### PX-04 · A Night Off

1. **ID and title:** PX-04 · A Night Off
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Delphine Rowe, 31, the most famous voice in the country. Sponsored by Tina Grandison, her first sponsorship. Delphine is first named by Cyrus (PX-10).
3. **Desire:** one ordinary night, unrecognized, at a family dinner where she's nobody's guest of honour.
4. **Offering:** she'll do every dish.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Bunny and Grace Okafor and their family. They agree to host a stranger; only Bunny knows who she is.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** fame. She can't walk into any room as herself, and money can't buy anonymity. The Veil would make this trivial, which is one reason the Veil is held back.
7. **Complication:** the only family Sloane now trusts with a secret is the one whose table leaked hers (S1-54).
8. **Instant interest:** a superstar wishing for the most ordinary thing in the world.
9. **Interesting people:** Delphine hasn't bought her own groceries since she was nineteen, and she's funny about her own fame. Grace is the matriarch who sees everything and says the kind thing.
10. **What Sloane does:** asks "What do you want?" Disguises her in her own closet (a wig, the Halo, last-season jeans, a cardigan). Has Gideon teach her a walk. Bans singing. Calls Bunny: "Can your table keep a secret, Mr. Okafor?" "It can now."
11. **Consent and agency:** the family agrees to host a guest. Bunny agrees to keep the secret. Delphine decides how much to reveal. No capture.
12. **Surprising turn:** Marcus plays a deep cut and she harmonizes for two bars. The yard goes still. Grace: "Pass the plate, baby."
13. **Outcome:** succeeds.
14. **Payoff:** a night of being nobody; the potato salad gag; Grace's unspoken kindness.
15. **Lingering consequence:** she attends the Grand Exchange in the wig, unrecognized (S1-72). Bunny is redeemed. Tina's sponsorship shows the sponsors' circle working.
16. **Recurring guests:** Delphine (could return as a patron); Grace (new); Marcus.
17. **Invention / visual:** the Halo (new): a floating hood-dryer ring that hums like a salon on a Saturday.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-19 (named), S1-62 *A Night Off* (GE−23, 1:30), S1-64 *Potato Salad* (GE−20, 2:00). Paid off in S1-72 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** MED and MED.
20. **Season arc:** Bunny's redemption after the leak (S1-54); Marcus healing after Sunset; Tina as a sponsor supports Alternative A.

### PX-05 · One Dance, Owed

1. **ID and title:** PX-05 · One Dance, Owed (a Recall)
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Mavis Odom, 79 (80 by S1-51), a fruit seller at the covered market. No sponsor: a Recall concerns an obligation that already exists.
3. **Desire:** the one dance Richard pledged her in 1986, at a time of her choosing.
4. **Offering:** past consideration. The night Nadine was born, Mavis drove Richard and his wife to the hospital in her fruit truck. A brass Pledge token records the promise.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Richard. A Recall can't compel him; the Pledge records a promise but can't enforce it.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** a promise isn't a contract. Richard never danced with her because she never asked, and she wants the dance at the Crescent Room, in front of everyone.
7. **Complication:** Richard's knee is already failing (surgery comes at S1-66). And the Saturday she chooses is the night of the disclosure.
8. **Instant interest:** a forty-year-old debt, paid with a dance.
9. **Interesting people:** Mavis forgets nothing, forgives most things, and sells Sloane too many oranges. Richard is king of the floor, and vain about it.
10. **What Sloane does:** takes the token like a jewel; carries the Recall to her father; arranges the music with Clarence; makes sure Ada is there as witness.
11. **Consent and agency:** Richard chooses to honour it. Mavis chooses the time. The witness records the settlement with everyone's consent.
12. **Surprising turn:** Mavis leads, and Mavis dips Richard, not the other way round. While the whole room watches the dance, Richard whispers to Bunny. The disclosure happens under its cover.
13. **Outcome:** succeeds. "Settled, with interest."
14. **Payoff:** joy on a dance floor, and a trick that rewards a second viewing.
15. **Lingering consequence:** Mavis comes back with her own request (PX-08) and receives a key (S1-69).
16. **Recurring guests:** Mavis is the season's recurring patron.
17. **Invention / visual:** the Pledge token, refined from the bible's Pledge/Witness: a brass token stamped with the year. The image is Mavis holding it high as she crosses the floor.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-12 *Paused* (GE−80, extended 1:00 → 1:30) and S1-20 *The Crescent Room* (GE−70, 1:30 → 1:45). Paid off in S1-69 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** S1-12 MED; S1-20 HIGH (unchanged tier).
20. **Season arc:** direct but innocent. The dance is the cover for the disclosure (S1-20). Mavis never knows.

### PX-06 · The Tuesday Table

1. **ID and title:** PX-06 · The Tuesday Table
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Lorna Shaw, 70, Gideon's mother. Sponsored by her pastor, not her son.
3. **Desire:** to lose at spades, fairly, once. Everyone at church lets her win, and she's sure of it.
4. **Offering:** the hand-carved wooden spade that has stood on her mantel since 1974, and Sunday dinner for a month.
5. **Who grants or declines:** whoever sits down to play: Nadine and Ada.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** nobody at church will play her honestly; you can't buy an honest opponent; and no registry can tell Sloane who plays spades.
7. **Complication:** the best player in the city is Sloane's own sister, who doesn't take favours from Sloane.
8. **Instant interest:** a seventy-year-old woman who suspects she's being allowed to win.
9. **Interesting people:** Lorna is funny, proud and competitive. Nadine is the hospital's chief operating officer and a night-shift spades shark. Ada counts everything.
10. **What Sloane does:** learns from a night nurse who the best player is. Walks into a 3 a.m. break room in opera gloves. Asks her sister properly, through her teeth.
11. **Consent and agency:** Nadine agrees only when she's asked properly. Lorna chooses to play. Nobody throws a trick.
12. **Surprising turn:** Lorna wins anyway. "I asked to lose fairly. I never promised to."
13. **Outcome:** changes. The patron gets her answer by winning.
14. **Payoff:** ruthless, hilarious cards; Black church social life; a new rivalry.
15. **Lingering consequence:** Nadine and Lorna keep playing on Tuesdays (S1-85: "Please don't."). It seeds the sisters' "ask me properly" before S1-61 ("Nadine. I need you.") and S1-75.
16. **Recurring guests:** Lorna (already a guest at S1-72) gains a story of her own, and Nadine gains a rival.
17. **Invention / visual:** none. The Constellation's limit is the joke.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-18 *Undefeated* (GE−72, 1:15), S1-24 *Spades* (GE−67, a Tuesday, 1:30). Paid off in S1-83 and S1-85.
19. **Production complexity:** MED and MED.
20. **Season arc:** the sisters' "ask me properly" rhythm before S1-61. Gideon's mother walks into The Exchange on her own before he brings her on his arm (S1-72).

### PX-07 · Lane Four

1. **ID and title:** PX-07 · Lane Four
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Dee Whitlock, 58, who set the city's 200-metre record at seventeen, from lane four, in 1985. Sponsored by her college roommate, Honora Vance.
3. **Desire:** on paper, to coach Zuri Bankole's start. In truth, to race her.
4. **Offering:** her start, which she has never explained in any interview.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Zuri Bankole, 19 (and, formally, Zuri's coach).
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** Zuri's coach refuses every outside offer, and a legend can't just turn up at a track and challenge a teenager.
7. **Complication:** the sponsor filed her objection to Kai the day before. Sloane has to accept a referral from the woman opposing her.
8. **Instant interest:** a record-holder chasing the girl who is chasing her record.
9. **Interesting people:** Dee is dry, proud and still competitive. Zuri is polite, impatient and tired of being mentored. Honora is fair.
10. **What Sloane does:** sets up a mentoring session, which is the wrong read. She realizes she misread the woman, and arranges the dawn race instead.
11. **Consent and agency:** Zuri chooses the race. The archive footage is Dee's own, used with her consent. The joggers are passers-by.
12. **Surprising turn:** "Everybody wants to mentor me. Nobody wants to race me." Dee was never there to teach.
13. **Outcome:** changes, then succeeds. They race; Zuri wins; they keep racing on Fridays.
14. **Payoff:** a dawn race beside a line of light, and Dee laughing on the infield.
15. **Lingering consequence:** Honora and Sloane share a thermos without mentioning Kai (S1-40), the civility that S1-63 will need. Dee and Zuri receive keys (S1-69) and race through a line dance at S1-74.
16. **Recurring guests:** Dee and Zuri, as a pair.
17. **Invention / visual:** the Echo Track, refined from the bible's Echo Archive: footage of a past performance laid over the real place where it happened, as a line of light. It shows only what was recorded, and only with consent.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-37 *Lane Four* (GE−54, 1:45), S1-40 *Dawn* (GE−50, 1:30). Paid off in S1-69, S1-74 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** MED and MED.
20. **Season arc:** shows Honora as fair (her objection is separate from the house) and builds the civility that precedes her agreement in S1-63.

### PX-08 · Fourteen Hundred Hats

1. **ID and title:** PX-08 · Fourteen Hundred Hats
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Mavis Odom, 80 today, a returning patron. A settled Recall gives her standing; she needs no sponsor.
3. **Desire:** every one of her fourteen hundred church hats on the right head, all worn on the same Saturday, before she moves to a smaller apartment.
4. **Offering:** the hats. Her one condition: each taker tells her where they'll wear it first.
5. **Who grants or declines:** each person who takes a hat.
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** she can't give away fourteen hundred hats alone. A sale would scatter them to strangers who don't care. She wants them worn, all at once.
7. **Complication:** everyone wants Sloane to choose for them, and she won't.
8. **Instant interest:** a truck backs onto Ferry Street and drops its tailgate: hatboxes to the roof.
9. **Interesting people:** Mavis again, and the market's own characters: a skateboarder, a barber, a girl of six.
10. **What Sloane does:** arranges the street, the market and the truck. Refuses to match a single hat: "They'll choose better than I would." Then: "Ask your sister."
11. **Consent and agency:** every taker chooses their own hat. Sloane withholds her verdict on purpose.
12. **Surprising turn:** the refusal is the point, and Mavis approves of it. "They picked better than you would have." "I know."
13. **Outcome:** succeeds.
14. **Payoff:** a river of hats down Ferry Street; community joy.
15. **Lingering consequence:** hats appear at the Grand Exchange, with Mavis in one of her own (S1-72). Giving up the verdict is dramatized without a speech.
16. **Recurring guests:** Mavis.
17. **Invention / visual:** none. The hats are the spectacle.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-51 *Market Day* (GE−35, a Saturday, extended 1:00 → 1:45). Paid off in S1-69, S1-72 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** HIGH (up from MED: crowd, drumline, hundreds of hats).
20. **Season arc:** thematic. Sloane refuses a verdict the day after Gideon is cleared (S1-49) and the day before she posts *What do you want?* (S1-52).

### PX-09 · Sunset

1. **ID and title:** PX-09 · Sunset
2. **Patron / sponsor:** Marcus Okafor, Royce's young assistant and Bunny's grandson. Sponsored by Royce: "Consider it an apology for the toast."
3. **Desire:** to propose to Renée Coker on the Halcyon House balcony at sunset, with a recorded message from her best friend.
4. **Offering:** six months of weekends working sponsored cases.
5. **Who grants or declines:** Renée Coker. For the pieces of the evening: Sloane (the venue) and Abena Mensah (her recorded message).
6. **Why ordinary routes fail:** Halcyon's balcony isn't for hire. Abena lives in Accra and can't travel, and the Interval is a professional device. The one ordinary route, simply asking Renée, is the one he skips.
7. **Complication:** unknown to Sloane, the patron is the leak's source. He repeated Bunny's table talk to Royce at GE−66. The audience has only a hint (S1-28).
8. **Instant interest:** a beautiful proposal setup that the audience senses is wrong before Sloane does.
9. **Interesting people:** Marcus is eager and wants to look big. Renée is private and precise. Abena is warm and far away.
10. **What Sloane does:** accepts Royce's referral and secures the balcony, the trio and the recorded message. She never asks whether Renée wants a public proposal. That is her mistake.
11. **Consent and agency:** Renée's consent is the one nobody sought. She uses it by saying no and leaving. Abena's message was recorded with consent and is never played.
12. **Surprising turn:** Renée refuses. "Everybody I love knew before I did. You asked all of them. You never asked me." Then: "You repeat everything to look big, Marcus."
13. **Outcome:** fails.
14. **Payoff:** heartbreak played straight, and Sloane owning her share: "I asked everyone but her."
15. **Lingering consequence:** a week later Marcus confesses to his grandfather, as reported in S1-54. Sloane's *What do you want?* (S1-52) gains a second reason. After the Grand Exchange, Renée proposes to Marcus in a laundromat (S1-81), and he tells no one.
16. **Recurring guests:** Marcus and Renée.
17. **Invention / visual:** the Interval silk, refined from the bible's Interval: a hanging silk panel holding a stored, consented message that plays on a cue. It cannot respond. The image is Abena's face waiting mid-smile for a yes that never comes.
18. **Placement and duration:** S1-45 *The Chair Next to Gideon* (GE−44, 1:30, combined and moved), S1-47 *Sunset* (GE−38, 1:45). Paid off in S1-54, S1-81 and S1-83.
19. **Production complexity:** S1-45 LOW; S1-47 MED.
20. **Season arc:** strong. It gives Marcus's confession a cause (S1-54). It repeats Sloane's first failure in a smaller key: at Y−9 she brought a father and son together before the son was ready. It doesn't explain the leak, because Marcus's repetition predates the request by three weeks.

### PX-10 · The Coat

1. **ID and title:** PX-10 · The Coat

10. **What Sloane does:** tries the coat on, takes it off, and declines. She also turns down the no-strings version: "a gift that arrives with a request is still a fee."

## 05 — Invention and Experience Roster

> **PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Every invention in the bible is a working concept (Bible 06). The Ember and the Threshold come from the hybrid, which the founder has not approved. The Hush and the Halo are new. Nothing here is canon.

### Principle

Wonder first. Understanding follows. Attachment makes it matter. Each invention appears as an image before anyone explains it. Each solves only part of a request and has a hard limit the story respects. None of them fixes an earlier conflict after the fact (Bible 06: "No retroactive magic fix").

Four of the ten scenarios use no invention at all: PX-01 (a custom), PX-06 (a card table), PX-08 (hats) and PX-10 (a coat). The world feels engineered, not gadget-driven.

### The inventions in use

**Pledge token** (PX-05)
- *What it is:* a brass token stamped with the year a promise was made, linked to a verifiable record of the terms.
- *What it allows:* a promise made decades ago can be produced, read and recognized by both parties.
- *First experience:* S1-12. Mavis, the fruit seller, sets a brass token worn smooth and stamped 1986 on her scale. Sloane takes it like a jewel.
- *Why it's appealing:* it is jewellery and a receipt at once. Mavis walking into the Crescent Room holding it high (S1-20) is the shot.
- *Relation to the request:* it proves Richard owes her a dance. It cannot make him dance.
- *Consent limit:* it records an agreement but can't compel fulfilment or prove sincerity (Bible 06). Richard says yes because he wants to.
- *Feasibility and cost:* one practical prop. No VFX. Low.

**Relay paper** (PX-02, and Celestine's note in S1-86)
- *What it is:* paper that stays blank to everyone except the reader it was written for.
- *What it allows:* a private, authenticated note that can be handed across a crowded bus.
- *First experience:* S1-15. Sloane has Tina write her letter on it, and the page goes blank. The payoff is S1-34: the card travels hand to hand around a sewing circle, unreadable, until Tina's writing blooms in Ophelia's hand.
- *Why it's appealing:* handwriting appearing in someone's hand is intimate and simple.
- *Relation to the request:* it gets Tina's invitation to Ophelia without exposing her. It doesn't make Ophelia say yes.
- *Consent limit:* it proves who sent the note, not that the note is true or kind (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* an ink-reveal insert, practical or a simple composite. Low. It resolves the bible's "Relay" naming question by making the paper the physical object and the Relay the protocol.

**Constellation** (PX-06, shown by its limit)
- *What it is:* the bible's permission-based map of introductions and obligations.
- *What it allows on screen:* nothing, which is the point. "No registry can tell Sloane who plays spades." A night nurse can (S1-18).
- *Why it's appealing:* a world where people still know things the system doesn't.
- *Consent limit:* it doesn't reveal undocumented links (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* none. It is a line of dialogue.

**Echo Track** (PX-07)
- *What it is:* footage of a past performance laid over the real place where it happened, as a moving line of light.
- *What it allows:* a person can race, walk or dance beside a recorded version of themselves.
- *First experience:* S1-40, at dawn. Lane four lights up with a pale line that runs Dee's 1985 record stride for stride, drawn from archive footage of her own race. "That's you?" "That was me."
- *Why it's appealing:* light on a track at dawn, and a record you can see.
- *Relation to the request:* it gives the race a third runner. It doesn't decide who wins.
- *Consent limit:* it plays only what was recorded, with the athlete's consent. It can't recreate an unrecorded moment (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* one light-streak VFX element over a real location. Medium. The technique is reusable.

**Interval silk** (PX-09)
- *What it is:* a hanging silk panel that holds a stored, consented message and plays it on a cue.
- *What it allows:* someone who can't be present can be part of a moment, asynchronously.
- *First experience:* S1-47. Abena's recorded message is set to bloom the moment Renée says yes. Renée doesn't. On the silk, Abena waits mid-smile.
- *Why it's appealing:* the most romantic object in the season becomes its saddest image.
- *Relation to the request:* it brings the best friend to the proposal. It can't change the answer.
- *Consent limit:* stored, consented content only. It is not live and cannot respond (Bible 06).
- *Feasibility and cost:* one held frame projected on silk. Low to medium.

**The Hush** (PX-03)
- *What it is:* a sound field only the people inside its circle can hear, with smaller private pockets within it.
- *What it allows:* a rooftop party with music everywhere for the guests and silence for the neighbours. Two people in a pocket can talk privately in a crowd.
- *First experience:* S1-50. Silence, then music, as a guest steps over the line. The street below hears nothing.
- *Why it's appealing:* it can be shot as pure sound design and it creates a new kind of party.
- *Relation to the request:* it is how two rival radio hosts end up talking without knowing who the other is. Inside it they recognize each other's voices.
- *Consent limit:* it can't record and can't be heard from outside. Everyone inside chooses to step in.
- *Feasibility and cost:* sound design plus actor reactions. A faint ring of light at the edge is optional. Low to medium.

**The Halo** (PX-04)
- *What it is:* a floating hood-dryer ring that hums like a salon on a Saturday.
- *What it allows:* a set or a wig styled in minutes, in anyone's closet.
- *First experience:* S1-62. Delphine's disguise is set under it in Sloane's closet.
- *Why it's appealing:* it puts the salon at the centre of the season's technology instead of a lab.
- *Relation to the request:* it helps the disguise. The disguise works because of Sloane's craft and Gideon's lesson in walking like someone with nowhere to be.
- *Consent limit:* it styles hair. It doesn't change a face or an identity.
- *Feasibility and cost:* one hero prop with a hover rig or simple float VFX. Medium. It could recur in Sloane's own routine; that's optional and not in the map.

**The Ember** and **the Threshold** stay exactly as the hybrid proposed. The Ember is the Living Legacy's object (copper *no*, gold *yes*; S1-01, S1-26, S1-58, S1-82). The Threshold is Kai's one night of access, with Honora's consent (S1-61, S1-63). This sprint doesn't change them.

### Held back

- **The Veil** (bible): consensual identity shielding. It would make PX-04 trivial. A famous woman who could switch her face off wouldn't need a family's discretion. Hold it for a request where shielding creates trouble instead of removing it.
- **Glint** (new idea, held): a small light on a lapel that tells any camera in the room whether the wearer may be recorded. It would turn devices-down (PX-01) from a custom into a technology. Hold it.

### Before any of these is approved

Bible 06 requires a full dossier per device: purpose, mechanism and speculative assumption, inputs and outputs, limits, permissions, failure modes, price and access class, cultural effects, novelty, effect on earlier episodes, and the tension it introduces. This section is a short-form proposal, not that dossier. None of these inventions has been tested visually. That is a production risk (§11).
