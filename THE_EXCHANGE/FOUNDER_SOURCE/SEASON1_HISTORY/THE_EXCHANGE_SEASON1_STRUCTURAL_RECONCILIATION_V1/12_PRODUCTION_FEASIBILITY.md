## 2. Compelling on paper versus producible now

The screen test's lessons, applied to the architecture:

| Lesson | Where it bites | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue needs breathing room | Rapid exchanges such as S1-61 (*"Say it again, slower." "No."*) and S1-24's table talk | Keep the lines and give each one a beat of reaction. Budget runtime for silence, not more words |
| Multiple speakers increase complexity | S1-20 (Mavis, Richard, Nadine, Sloane, Ada), S1-24 (four at a table), S1-38 (makers), S1-72 | No more than two speaking faces per shot. Cut to singles for lines, and to wides only for music and reaction |
| Facial identity drift is real | Recurring patrons (Mavis, Tina, Ophelia, Delphine, Dee, Zuri, Tad, Yaz, Lorna, Royce); the young Vivienne and Celestine (S1-58) | Extend face locks (FD-18) to recurring patrons before their first speaking release (U-6) |
| Shot geography must be coherent | Parties: S1-04, S1-20, S1-28, S1-40, S1-50, S1-72 to S1-79 | Map every party as three fixed pockets (bar, floor, edge) plus one hero position, and keep them identical across releases in the same venue |
| Wardrobe continuity follows the fictional day | GE0 (13 releases), GE−92 (5 releases), S1-40 (two fictional days, one outfit) | Lock one look per character per fictional day, using the chronology table in BASELINE V1 |
| One environment needs consistent reverse angles | Sloane's office (9 releases), Richard's house (9), the registry room (7) | Build each recurring room once with a fixed reverse-angle pair, and reuse it |
| Finalize audio before lip-sync | Every speaking release | Record and lock ElevenLabs dialogue per release before any speaking shot is generated |
| Crowds and parties need planning | 15 HIGH and VERY HIGH releases, mostly crowds | See section 3 |

## 3. Crowd and party releases

**Ratings:** S1-20 HIGH, S1-40 HIGH, S1-50 HIGH, S1-04 / S1-28 / S1-72 / S1-74 / S1-77 VERY HIGH.

**How to keep the prestige look:**
- **Crowd as plate, principals as pockets.** Generate the room and crowd as a background plate per venue. Place principals in foreground pockets, and reuse the plate across every release in that venue: Halcyon, the River Hall, the Crescent Room.
- **Dance through inserts and silhouettes.** Show feet, hands, skirts and shoulders, then cut to a wide silhouette against the band. Full-body partner choreography in a single generated shot is the riskiest image in the season.
- **Music-only beats.** These need no lip-sync, and Option A already leans on them. Nadine's dance in S1-74 is *music only*, and in S1-20 *"we hear only the band"* over the whisper.
- **Light as spectacle.** The Echo Track (S1-40), the Hush circle (S1-50) and the lanterns (S1-77) are light effects. They are cheaper and more controllable than extra crowd.

**The two new Nights:**
- **S1-20 *Nineteen Eighty-Six*** reuses the Crescent Room set (S1-09, S1-35). Its cost is wardrobe: 1986 period dress for principals and a crowd plate. Keep speaking roles to five (Mavis, Richard, Nadine, Sloane, Ada), and stage the dip as a held two-shot.
- **S1-40 *The Breakfast Dance*** needs one new interior (the Riverside Pavilion) and reuses the river track exterior. Shoot the breakfast as tabletop inserts, and the walk to the bleachers as silhouettes against the dawn. The race is mostly Dee, Zuri and a line of light.

## 6. Manageable shot structures

1. **The two-hander:** a fixed room, two singles and one two-shot (the office, the car, Richard's porch). The workhorse; it covers most STORY releases.
2. **The montage without lip-sync:** S1-01, S1-17, S1-69, S1-81.
3. **The music-only beat:** S1-70, S1-74, the dance in S1-20.
4. **The freeze-frame introduction:** cheap and on brand (FD-20). None added.
5. **The party in three pockets:** for every Night.

**Option B's effect.** Its merges put continuous scenes in one set: Richard's front room into his porch, and the study into the memory. That saves setups. The cost is longer single releases, which are harder to keep identity-stable across many shots.

**Bottom line.** Neither map is producible end to end with the current workflow until three things exist: patron face locks, crowd-plate tests and one partner-dance test. The recommendation is to test S1-20's dip and S1-40's bleacher walk early, because they are the new risks this package introduces.
