# 02 — Sloane's Step-Back: Three Alternatives and a Recommendation

> **NONCANONICAL. PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** This file reassesses decision S-1. It recommends an approach but implements nothing as canon. Release IDs are BASELINE V1 (S1-nn); Option A keeps them. Option B equivalents are in `10_FULL_RELEASE_CHANGELOG.md`.

## 1. The question

**Why is Sloane changing her relationship with The Exchange now, this season, and not last year or next?**

The founder supports Alternative A (she gives up the verdict, not the craft) as a direction. The founder has not approved its emotional motivation or its finale. The answer must be character-driven, credible, season-long and consequential. It must not be burnout, retirement, personal failure alone, restructuring alone, or loss of joy, and giving up sponsorship must not count as the payoff by itself.

### What BASELINE V1 already says

- S1-05, voice-over: *"Every year I tell myself I'll stop deciding who gets what. Every year I'm very good at it."*
- S1-10, to Gideon: *"I'm not leaving. I'll still host. I'll still take the impossible requests. I just don't want my opinion of someone to be their whole life anymore."*
- S1-75, on stage: this is the last Living Legacy she chooses. From next year the makers choose the visions and the patrons she has sponsored vouch for newcomers. *"Just ask me properly."*

That is Alternative A as written. It says clearly **what** she gives up. It is thinner on **why now**, and its public payoff is a change in who decides. On its own, that risks reading as the administrative transfer the founder has ruled out.

### Pressures already in the material (every alternative draws on these)

1. **Ten years.** Vivienne died ten years ago, at 55. Sloane first took the chair at 23, stepped back during the Quiet Years and resumed at 28 (FD-12). She has been back for five years. Her mother's envelope, *C. R.*, has lain face down in the desk for ten years (S1-07).
2. **Kai's note (S1-03, S1-05).** A man she excluded asks for the one thing she can't decide cleanly, because it touches her mother. Later he says: *"You excluded me once without asking me why. Now you're doing it again"* (S1-32).
3. **Richard's knee.** It troubles him from S1-20, it gives in S1-54, and he has surgery at GE−14 (S1-66). The family's centre of gravity is ageing in front of her.
4. **Gideon's offer abroad.** He has had it for a year and reveals it in S1-85. Her protector has a life he has been postponing for her.
5. **Nadine.** *"When everybody needs something from you, I get told last"* (S1-31). Nadine is planning a sabbatical (S1-85).

### What each alternative must avoid

| Trap | How the alternatives avoid it |
|---|---|
| Burnout or lost joy | In every version she still loves the work; S1-10 and S1-75 keep her taking impossible requests. |
| Retirement | No version ends with her leaving The Exchange. A3 keeps leaving possible and unresolved. |
| Failure alone | The breach (S1-28 onwards) is a pressure, not the cause. |
| Restructuring alone | The makers' choice (S1-75) is a consequence in every version, never the payoff. |
| Sponsorship given up = resolution | The emotional payoff is personal (S1-74, S1-78, S1-80), not procedural. |

## 2. A1 — The verdict, not the craft (strengthened, same mechanics)

**Motivation.** Her word decides who comes through the door, and a verdict lasts. Ten years ago she excluded Kai without asking him why, and that one decision became his whole life. When his request arrives through Celestine, she sees the cost of her own power on a face she knows. The FD-02 strand that leads is *the weight of her word*.

**Setup.** S1-03 (Kai at the foot of the steps: *"Not tonight, Kai"*), S1-05 (the voice-over), S1-08 (the Living Legacy notebook she has been designing for a year), S1-10 (she tells Gideon first).

**Midseason evolution.** The breach (S1-28) makes her verdicts public property. S1-32 makes her hear what her exclusion did. The Number 9 (S1-34) and The Coat (S1-19) show her deciding brilliantly, and S1-60 shows the code holding against what she wants (Kai's reinstatement denied, FD-11).

**Relationship consequences.** Kai: she apologizes for the act, not just the effect. Gideon: he guards the door she is opening. Nadine: hurt at being told last; the verdict story barely reaches her. Richard: his joy at *"my baby's coming home"* reads as a misunderstanding.

**Living Legacy.** Its purpose is to move the choice from one judge to the makers. Celestine and Kai's *yes* is the last vision Sloane chooses.

**Patron requests.** Unchanged. She still facilitates; only sponsorship of new members changes.

**Gideon, Nadine, Richard, Vivienne.** Vivienne's legacy is read as an institution to reform. The two estranged collaborators rhyme with the verdict theme. Richard and Nadine stay secondary to the institutional story.

**Grand Exchange.** S1-75's announcement is the climax, applauded in public. The private disappointment is Celestine's *"The rest is mine"* (S1-78).

**Final payoff.** *"Just ask me properly."* She keeps the craft and the room laughs with her.

**Later seasons.** Clean. Season 2 opens with the makers choosing, Sloane still taking impossible requests, and a stranger at the door (S1-83).

**Exact map changes.** None. A1 is BASELINE V1. To strengthen it, tie the S1-05 voice-over explicitly to Kai, and add one line to S1-32: Sloane admits she never asked him why. Both are optional line edits, and Option A does not include them.

**Why the audience should care.** Watching a powerful woman notice her own power is satisfying. It is a public, adult virtue, but it is cooler than the founder's brief: it asks the audience to admire her more than to ache for her.

**Assessment.** Credible, season-long, and the cheapest option. Its weakness is emotional temperature: the finale's change is mostly procedural, and the family scenes in Arcs 5 and 6 run on a different engine from her motive.

## 3. A2 — The craft survives, the personal burden changes

**Motivation.** Sloane has made herself the person who decides for everyone, because being needed is the only kind of love she trusts. She decides for patrons, for Kai, for Nadine (told last), and for herself. Nobody asks her what she wants, and she makes sure nobody has to. The Lockwoods have done this for three generations:

- Vivienne gave the Ember away without asking Celestine (S1-58).
- Richard told Bunny without asking Sloane (S1-55: *"You decided for me." "I did. Like your mother. Like you."*).
- Sloane excluded Kai without asking him why (S1-32).
- Vivienne's last letter says it outright: *"I know I'm deciding for you again"* (S1-86).

The FD-02 strand that leads is *loved-or-useful*.

**Why now.** The pressures converge on one question she has avoided for ten years: what does she want, separate from what everyone needs from her? These are the anniversary, the envelope, Kai's request, Richard's knee and Gideon's postponed life. The Living Legacy has been in her notebook for a year (S1-08). She can design a tradition that stops her deciding for others; she can't yet stop deciding for herself.

**Setup.**
- S1-07: *"You look like you're carrying something." "Pastries."*
- S1-10: in Option A, Gideon asks *"And what do you want?"* and she says *"Dessert."*
- S1-14: Richard: *"My baby's coming home."*
- S1-31: *"I get told last."*

**Midseason evolution.**
- S1-44: the *Two Names* card. She can't ask the two people she loves most the one question.
- S1-49: Gideon says *"Ask me."* She finally does, and cries for the first time since her mother died.
- S1-52: she sends Celestine the question her family never asked: *What do you want?*
- S1-55: Richard names the pattern.
- S1-61: she asks for help for the first time: *"Nadine. I need you."*
- S1-66 and S1-67: she chooses her father's bedside, and the rehearsal works without her.

**Relationship consequences.**
- Nadine stops being told last and starts being asked (S1-61, then S1-74 in Option A).
- Richard's flaw becomes the mirror he holds up to her (S1-55).
- Gideon is free to consider his own offer (S1-85): she says *"Go if you want,"* and means it.
- With Celestine, Sloane gives her the envelope instead of deciding what it says (S1-78).

**Living Legacy.** Unchanged mechanically. Its meaning shifts: Sloane designs it to stop deciding for others, and the season makes her learn that the same applies at home.

**Patron requests.** Unchanged, and stronger: she keeps the craft because she loves it, not because it keeps her needed. The requests show her at her best. In The Coat (S1-19) she turns down the coat she has wanted for fifteen years, because *"a gift that arrives with a request is still a fee."* In the Number 9 (S1-34) she lets a blank card travel hand to hand so that nobody has to say who it's for.

**Gideon, Nadine, Richard, Vivienne.** All four become part of the motive instead of bystanders to it. Vivienne's legacy becomes a family pattern to break, not an institution to reform. FD-06's private truth carries the weight.

**Grand Exchange.** In public it is a triumph (S1-75 unchanged). In private there are three moments:
- In S1-74 (Option A), Nadine asks Sloane to dance, properly. For one song the room runs without her and nothing breaks.
- In S1-78, Celestine reveals that Vivienne once asked her what she wanted.
- In S1-80 (Option A), Richard asks: *"What do you want, baby?"* She thinks about it for real: *"The impossible ones. I still want the impossible ones."*

**Final payoff.** She chooses the craft *for herself*. The private disappointment stays intact: *"The rest is mine"* (S1-78), Gideon's offer (S1-85), and Nadine's sabbatical.

**Later seasons.** The strongest of the three. She is freer, not smaller. Season 2 can test whether she can be asked, as well as whether she can deliver. *"Show her in"* (S1-83) keeps the door open.

**Exact map changes.** Three line additions, all in Option A and marked S-1 dependent: S1-10, S1-74 and S1-80. Every other beat listed above already exists in BASELINE V1. No release is added.

**Why the audience should care.** Everyone knows someone who is everybody's person and nobody's priority. Her change costs her something and gives her something, and the audience gets to see her receive.

**Assessment.** The most emotionally consequential option, and nearly free on the map. **Risk:** it leans on FD-06 (Vivienne's letter). If the founder keeps Vivienne's truth unresolved, S1-86's line has to survive in some form.

## 4. A3 — Contemplated departure, genuinely unresolved

**Motivation.** She genuinely considers leaving The Exchange, not to rest but because another life is offered and she is good at it. Provisional device: Tina Grandison, who edits the city's best fashion magazine (S1-15), offers Sloane the creative directorship. The offer is real, glamorous and on brand: a career where her taste is the product and nobody's life depends on her opinion. The FD-02 strand that leads is *her mother's withdrawal*: Vivienne withdrew too, and Sloane has never been sure whether that was wisdom or flight.

**Setup.** S1-10, unchanged: *"I'm not leaving,"* which the audience is free to doubt. Tina's offer arrives after the Number 9 succeeds, which needs a new beat in S1-34 or S1-62.

**Midseason evolution.** One day in Tina's world needs a new release or a replacement, for example inside S1-62's closet sequence. The breach makes leaving tempting. FD-12 is the honest problem: she has stepped back once before. The season has to ask whether this time is different, or whether she is repeating the Quiet Years.

**Relationship consequences.** Gideon's offer abroad becomes a mirror: two people who could both leave. Nadine is wary (*"She's not—"*, S1-31). Richard is delighted, as he is in A1 and A2, but for the wrong reason.

**Living Legacy.** It becomes her succession plan. That pushes it toward the administrative finale the founder ruled out.

**Patron requests.** Each success is a reason to stay, which risks making the requests feel like an argument instead of an adventure.

**Gideon, Nadine, Richard, Vivienne.** Vivienne's withdrawal becomes the central question. Gideon's future becomes tied to Sloane's decision, which partly undoes FD-15's independent life.

**Grand Exchange.** She must announce something at S1-75, and *"I haven't decided"* is not a stage line. The likely outcome is that S1-75 stays as it is, and the real decision moves to the epilogue.

**Final payoff.** Unresolved: she turns down Tina, or defers. *"Show her in"* (S1-83) answers it in practice, but the audience can feel the series protecting itself.

**Later seasons.** The weakest. If she stays, it was a fake retirement. If she half-leaves, the series has to rebuild its centre. It can work only if Season 2 makes leaving a live question again.

**Exact map changes.** Larger than the others: Tina's offer (a line in S1-34 or S1-62), a day-in-the-other-life release (+1 or one replacement), a decision beat around S1-85, and possibly a rewrite of S1-75. Not built into either option map.

**Why the audience should care.** The suspense is real only if the audience believes she could go, and genre logic says she won't. Its strongest gift is honesty: she isn't sure what she wants.

**Assessment.** Most faithful to the founder's original wording ("contemplated change"), but riskiest for renewability and closest to the fake-retirement trap. Its best element, real uncertainty, can be taken without its machinery.

## 5. Would a hybrid be stronger?

Yes. Each alternative has one element the others need:

- **A1** gives a visible, public consequence: the makers choose and the patrons vouch (S1-75). Without it, A2's change has no institutional shape.
- **A2** gives the reason and the heat: a woman learning to be asked. Without it, A1's payoff is procedural.
- **A3** gives honesty: she genuinely doesn't know what she wants until she is asked. Without it, A2 risks feeling pre-solved.

**The recommended hybrid (H):**
- **Spine:** A2. The burden that changes is deciding for everyone; the craft survives.
- **Visible consequence:** A1's mechanics, kept exactly as in S1-75.
- **Texture:** A3's honesty, without Tina's offer or a departure plot. She deflects the question (S1-10, *"Dessert."*) and answers it for real only at S1-80.

## 6. Recommendation (not implemented as canon)

**Recommend Hybrid H.** Option A threads it through three releases, each marked *S-1 dependent* in `04_EXPANDED_86_RELEASE_MAP.csv`:

| Release | Addition | Function |
|---|---|---|
| S1-10 *First Person* | Gideon: *"And what do you want?"* Sloane: *"Dessert."* He hears a door close. | Plants the question she can't answer about herself. |
| S1-74 *Noelle's Song* | Nadine crosses the floor and asks her to dance, properly. For one song the room runs without her. | She is asked, and lets herself receive. A music-only beat. |
| S1-80 *Pie* | Richard: *"What do you want, baby?"* *"The impossible ones. I still want the impossible ones."* *"Then that's yours."* | The payoff: she chooses the craft for herself. |

S1-75's line *"Just ask me properly"* now lands on a moment the audience has just seen: Nadine asked properly, one release earlier.

**Line it would retire:** none. Every V1 line stays. S1-10's *"I'm not leaving"* still plays, and the audience now hears that she hasn't answered the real question.

**Reversibility.** If the founder chooses A1, revert S1-10, S1-74 and S1-80 to their V1 text; nothing else depends on them. If the founder chooses A3, Option A's three lines still fit, and the extra A3 material (Tina's offer, a day in the other life, a decision beat) must be written and placed.

**Why this ending keeps the series active.** Sloane ends Season 1 still hosting, still facilitating, still taking impossible requests, and now doing it because she wants to. *"Show her in"* (S1-83) opens Season 2 on a stranger with something to offer. Her new vulnerability is that people can now ask things of *her*, and that is an engine, not an ending.
