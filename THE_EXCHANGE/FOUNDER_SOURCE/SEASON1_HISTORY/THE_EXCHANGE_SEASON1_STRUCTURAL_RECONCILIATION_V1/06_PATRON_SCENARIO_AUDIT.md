# 06 — Patron Scenario Audit (PX-01 to PX-10)

> **NONCANONICAL. PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** This is an editorial audit of the ten scenarios selected in BASELINE V1. Nothing here enters canon. Release IDs are BASELINE V1 (S1-nn); Option A keeps them.

## 1. Method

Each scenario is scored 1 to 5 on the sprint's fifteen criteria, as it stands in Option A. For the five scenarios Option A revises, the V1 total is shown underneath. The scores are editorial judgments, not measurements. They decided nothing alone; the special notes in section 3 carry equal weight.

The audit did not assume that all ten must stay. It asked of each one: would the season be worse without it, and is its screen time earned?

## 4. Special notes, scenario by scenario

**PX-01 One Private Hour: does it strengthen the season without forcing everything into the leak arc?**
- **Mostly yes.** The request (S1-09) and its payoff (S1-28) stand alone: a devices-down hour, two hundred young people hearing a big band, Royce dancing *"stiff, then, for eight bars, genuinely good."*
- The toast that turns the room (*"To Sloane Lockwood's last Grand Exchange, and whatever comes next"*) is Royce's own choice in his own club. It is the breach's public moment, not a consequence of the request.
- V1 tied the two together a second time: Royce sponsored Marcus *"as an apology for the toast"* (S1-45). Option A cuts that tie. Royce sponsors Marcus because he likes him and distrusts his own instincts: *"Don't let me near it; I'd put it on a screen."*
- Royce stays human opposition: generous, vain, self-aware, not a villain.

**PX-02 The Woman on the Number 9: preserve fashion, surprise and privacy.**
- Untouched. The blank Relay card travels hand to hand (S1-34), and Ophelia refuses the cover and counters with a closet swap.
- It is the season's best example of privacy-first worldbuilding and its strongest fashion request.

**PX-03 Off Air: can its Unannounced Exchange help the Nights gap?**
- Where it is (S1-50, GE−36), it already splits the second half.
- Moved earlier, it would leave a new 30-release gap and break the S1-49/S1-50 pairing (03, N3). Keep it in place.

**PX-04 A Night Off: warmth and comedy without implausible anonymity.**
- V1 relied on a superstar going unrecognized at a family cookout. Option A changes the premise: the family recognizes her and chooses not to say so.
- Grace looks one second too long. A cousin's lock screen shows Delphine's face, and he turns it over. Grace sends her home with leftovers: *"Come back when you're Delphine. You'll still wash."*
- At the Grand Exchange (S1-72), everybody recognizes her and nobody mentions it.
- The disguise comedy shrinks slightly (*"The wig is for the bus. The secret is for the table."*). The warmth grows, and the outcome becomes credible.

**PX-05 One Dance, Owed: joy, not machinery.**
- In V1 the dance existed partly so nobody would notice Richard's whisper. In Option A it is a night The Exchange throws for Mavis, *Nineteen Eighty-Six* (S1-20).
- The dance is complete in itself: Mavis dips Richard, and Ada writes *"Settled, with interest."* The whisper comes later, while Richard sits out a number beside Bunny.
- The season's most rewatchable moment survives: the audience takes the whisper for a joke between old friends until S1-55.

**PX-06 The Tuesday Table: cultural comedy, Gideon's family, Nadine's competitive streak.**
- Untouched. *Spades* (S1-24) is the season's purest comedy, and Nadine is *"magnificent."*
- Its weakness is visual (2): a fellowship hall and a card table. Fix it in production, not in the story: use top-down card shots, the church members pulling up chairs, and Lorna's Boston as a spectacle of hands.

**PX-07 Lane Four: cinematic and emotional.**
- Option A gives the race a world. A breakfast dance from 2 a.m. walks out to the bleachers at 5:40 in last night's clothes. The Echo Track runs Dee's 1985 stride in lane four, and Zuri passes both.
- Dee's real request stays the turn: to be beaten by someone worth it. *"Next Friday."*

**PX-08 Fourteen Hundred Hats: delight without a shallow montage.**
- V1's market day was a joyful montage, with Sloane refusing to match a single hat.
- Option A adds consequence through three destinations in Mavis's notebook: *"My graduation." "First day back at work." "My mother's homegoing, Saturday."* Mavis writes the last one slowly and gives that woman two.
- Sloane's contribution stays deliberately low (2). Her craft here is knowing when not to choose: *"They'll choose better than I would."*

**PX-09 Sunset: preserve the refusal and Renée's agency.**
- Untouched. Renée says no. Gideon blows out the candles one by one. *"I asked everyone but her."*
- Its consequences (Marcus confesses, Bunny tells Richard) stay exactly as written. No version makes the season happier by changing her answer.

**PX-10 The Coat: Sloane's temptation and refusal.**
- Untouched. She tries on the coat she has wanted for fifteen years, takes it off, and declines it twice.
- *"A gift that arrives with a request is still a fee."* The cake at her desk is the petty, human coda.
