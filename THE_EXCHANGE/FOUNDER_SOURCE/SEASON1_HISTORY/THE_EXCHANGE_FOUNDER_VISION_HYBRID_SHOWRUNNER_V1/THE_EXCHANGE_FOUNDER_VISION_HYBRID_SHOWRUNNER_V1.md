## 01 — Creative Mandate

**What the founder asked for (Sprint 4 §1–3, §9).** The strongest possible version of THE EXCHANGE, built on the founder's own Season 1 direction (storyline items A–G). It should borrow the *structural craftsmanship* of WORD OF MOUTH, not its events. It must protect what makes the property itself:
- glamour, warmth, humour and Black excellence;
- sophisticated romance and extraordinary social experiences;
- strong family relationships and beautiful everyday life;
- real suspense, in an aspirational world audiences want to visit.

**The two extremes to avoid.**
- **A:** beautiful people at glamorous parties with no dramatic engine.
- **B:** an oppressive mystery that dismantles the world's pleasure.

**How this hybrid answers.**
- **One spine, many lives.** The season's spine is one question: *can Sloane leave her mark without deciding for everyone again?* Every plot thread is a version of that question:
  - the tradition;
  - the estranged collaborators;
  - her mother's last act;
  - the leak.
  
  Around the spine, people live: a supper club for elders, a grandmother's gown, a teenager's song, a father who can't stop dancing, a hospital at night, a man growing tomatoes on a fire escape.
- **Joy in every arc.** Every arc contains a party, a meal, music or dancing (§17). Nothing waits for the finale.
- **Suspense from people, not plots.** Each mystery has a human source and a fair, planted answer (the writers'-room truth document).

**How this document was made.** The Cursor agent researched the sources and drafted an architecture (handoff §5B, "v0.3") before network failures stopped it. I verified the sources, pressure-tested the draft against the founder's items A–G and the seventeen dramatic-engine questions, kept what worked, and changed what didn't:

| # | Change from the v0.3 draft | Why |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Richard no longer urges Vivienne to give the Ember away.** The decision was hers alone. | The draft put *both* of the season's guilt reveals on Richard (the leak and the original wrong). That turns him into the hidden culprit the sprint forbids. Making the decision Vivienne's alone also gives her full agency. |
| C2 | **Kai attends through a Threshold, not as Celestine's personal guest.** | Invitations can't be transferred, and an excluded person can't be brought in privately. ## 05 — Hybrid Season One Logline

**Season working title:** **THE LIVING LEGACY** (alternative: *WHOSE LIGHT*)

> **Season logline.** Sloane Lockwood is planning the last Grand Exchange where she'll decide everything, crowned by a new tradition that makes people's unrealized dreams real. Its centerpiece needs two estranged artists back in the same room: the man she exiled, and the woman who has never forgiven the Lockwoods. Then a rival toasts "her last Grand Exchange" before she has told her own sister.

> **One-line engine.** A woman who arranges everyone's life learns, across two generations of her family, the difference between giving people things and asking them what they want.

**Season question:** Can Sloane leave her mark on The Exchange without deciding for everyone, one more time?

**Tagline candidates:** "Some lights you choose." / "The night is private. The legacy is yours."

## 06 — Complete Season One Synopsis

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Release IDs refer to §16. Dates count down to Grand Exchange day ("GE").

### Arc 1 — The Host (GE−92 to GE−80)

**Opening.**
- The city dresses for the night. On a balcony a woman dances in her kitchen. A neighbour's lens glows **copper**, the world's everyday *no*, and he lowers it; she raises a glass to him (LL-01).
- Sloane eats coconut cake before her makeup and apologizes to a dying fern (LL-02).
- At Halcyon House, Gideon handles the door. Kai Fontaine, whom Sloane excluded four years ago, waits at the foot of the steps and hands Gideon a note. The rival club owner Royce Bell tries to reach Sloane directly and is stopped (LL-03).
- Upstairs, Sloane does what she does best. She also tells a man in an orange jacket "Brave," and by morning his tailor has lost two clients: her word has become verdict (LL-04, LL-06).

**Inciting incident (LL-05).** Kai's note reads: *"Celestine has a vision she never built. She'll never ask. I'm asking."* An excluded man has no standing to ask anything.

**First decisions.**
- At the registry, Sloane opens the notebook where she has been designing a new tradition, *The Living Legacy*, and writes "Celestine" beside *Centerpiece* (LL-08).
- On the facing page is her mother's final ledger entry: a sponsored commission, beneficiary sealed, *WITHDRAWN BY FACILITATOR* nineteen days before Vivienne's death. "Mom never withdrew from anything."
- She tells Gideon, and only Gideon, that this will be her last Grand Exchange "as the one who decides" (LL-09).
- She hand-delivers an invitation to Celestine. It comes back unopened with a card: *No* (LL-10).
- She quietly pauses new personal sponsorships (LL-11).

### Arc 2 — Nominations (GE−77 to GE−64)

**Kai and Richard.**
- Sloane files a reinstatement petition so Kai can have standing, with a handwritten letter: "while it's still mine to set right" (LL-12).
- At Sunday breakfast she tells her father. Richard tries to hide his joy and fails: "My baby's coming home." He promises to keep it (LL-13).

**The tradition takes shape through people.**
- An 80-year-old recites her late father's dining-car menu from memory; the railroad never let him put his name on a plate (LL-14).
- Couriers deliver the makers' terms: from next year, *the choosing passes to this year's makers* (LL-15).
- A grandmother unfolds a 1981 gown sketch, "for being looked at" (LL-20).

**The disclosure (LL-16, LL-17).** That Saturday at the Crescent Room, Richard is king of the floor. He leans over and tells his oldest friend, Bunny, something, and both men laugh. Gideon is at dinner elsewhere.

**Kai and Celestine.**
- Kai teaches neighbourhood kids to build lights that bloom when you choose (LL-18).
- He knocks on the door of the last guests he hasn't apologized to. One, a retired judge, Honora Vance, asks him not to come back (LL-19).
- Sloane slips into Celestine's class. Celestine is lecturing on the Ember's lost gold state: "They only kept the no." Afterwards: "Kai doesn't get to nominate anybody. You saw to that." (LL-21)

**Sloane's wrong move (LL-22).** Then Sloane does something she shouldn't. She asks the registry to unseal the beneficiary of her mother's last commission. Ada: "Neither makes it yours. Only the beneficiary can consent, and I'm required to notify them, with your name on it." She signs anyway.

### Arc 3 — The Toast (GE−63 to GE−50)

**The toast (LL-23).** At the Salon, Royce's loud, open-capture club, Royce raises a glass "to Sloane Lockwood's last Grand Exchange, and whatever comes next." Sloane smiles and says nothing.

**The fallout.**
- She suspects Kai: her own letter hinted, and Royce has been courting him (LL-24).
- Nadine hears from a surgeon in her hospital corridor (LL-25) and comes to Sloane's door: "You told Daddy, and Gideon, and half the city before me." (LL-26)
- Sloane accuses Kai. He: "You excluded me once without asking me why. Now you're doing it again." (LL-27)
- Honora formally objects to Kai's reinstatement (LL-30).
- Over a beautiful dinner, the makers ask who will protect their work if she leaves. Clementine: "Then don't leave it to her. Leave it to us." (LL-31)

**Life goes on.**
- The Salon's open application night, warm and a little brutal (LL-28).
- A teenager's song is answered by an old bandleader's trumpet (LL-29).
- Richard teaches Gideon to hand-dance: "Lead like you're not guarding the door." (LL-32)
- Royce tries to hire Gideon and fails (LL-33).

### Arc 4 — Inference (GE−48 to GE−33), with the midpoint

**The romance begins in opposition (LL-34).** The city's consent counsel, Emmett Hale, rejects the centerpiece's design: "You've designed a beautiful way to say yes once. People change their minds."

**Midpoint (LL-36).** Over lunch, Royce shows off his inference:
- she paused sponsorships;
- Kai's petition is on the register;
- next year's date is on the venue's calendar;
- the makers' terms say the choosing passes to them.

Sloane is impressed. Then: "You said *last*. Nothing you inferred says last." He won't name his source. Only two people ever heard her say the word. That night she writes their names on a card and turns it face down (LL-37).

**Celestine opens, then closes.**
- Kai, unannounced, brings Celestine to a children's ward at night, where his lights help families choose. Nadine walks past and lets it happen. Celestine: "I'll hear you. Without her." (LL-38)
- Then the registry's notice reaches Celestine: Sloane has asked to unseal *her* commission. Celestine: "You invited me to make my *yes* so you could open my *no*." She leaves the project (LL-39). **The season's low point.**

**Recovery.**
- Gideon makes Sloane ask him. He didn't tell. She cries in the car for the first time since her mother died. "Then it was Daddy." (LL-40)
- She withdraws her request ("Withdrawn by facilitator," Ada says pointedly) and posts Celestine a single line: *What do you want?* (LL-42)

**Ordinary life between.**
- Emmett's fire-escape garden and the niece he's raising (LL-35).
- A street market where Sloane refuses to judge a hat (LL-41).
- Nadine and her husband dancing in their kitchen as she talks about the sabbatical she keeps refusing (LL-43).

### Arc 5 — Whose Light (GE−30 to GE−14)

**The breach is solved.**
- Bunny comes to Richard: his grandson, Royce's assistant, overheard it at Bunny's Sunday table (LL-44).
- Richard confesses to Sloane before she asks. He told because he was happy: "The work took your mother's attention from this family for thirty years. I was afraid it would take you." Sloane: "You decided for me." Richard: "I did. Like your mother. Like you." (LL-45) **Major reversal.**

**The past opens.**
- Sloane apologizes to Kai properly. He shows her the anonymous commission terms he has kept framed for ten years, in her mother's phrasing: "It was for Celestine." (LL-46)
- She turns over the envelope in her mother's desk: *C. R.* (LL-47)
- Richard tells the story, in his memory (LL-48). Thirty-one years ago Vivienne and Celestine designed the Ember together: copper *no*, gold *yes*. Vivienne alone gave it away, unattributed, and only the *no* was kept. Celestine learned from a newspaper. **Sloane exiled Kai for her mother's act without knowing it.**

**Consequences hold.**
- Honora's objection stands, and reinstatement is denied (LL-50).
- Celestine comes to Sloane for the first time, card in hand: "I want to finish the *yes*. With Kai. Without you deciding anything." She will ask for a Threshold, one night of access for Kai. Sloane recuses herself and asks her sister: "Nadine. I need you." (LL-51)
- Nadine walks Honora through the ward, and Honora agrees to one night: "Not reinstatement. And I'll be there." (LL-52)
- Emmett approves a fully revocable design (LL-53).
- Richard's knee surgery is moved to the morning of the first full rehearsal. Sloane chooses the hospital (LL-54). The rehearsal runs without her, and it works (LL-55).

### Arc 6 — The Grand Exchange (Grand Exchange day, near real time)

From cake at breakfast to cold pie on the back steps at 2:30 a.m.:
- Keys are delivered across the city; a gown is pinned; Richard rehearses his two words (LL-56–59).
- The River Hall fills: Royce comes as Sloane's guest, Honora sees Kai, Gideon brings his mother (LL-60).
- An 80-year-old reads her father's name on a menu for the first time (LL-61).
- The floor fills for a sixteen-year-old's song (LL-62).
- Sloane announces, on the record and on her own terms, that this is her last as their facilitator, not their host. She thanks Nadine first (LL-63).
- Fifteen minutes before midnight, the old rig's memory of the first Night threatens the centerpiece. Celestine and Kai decide *together* to erase it (LL-64).
- **At midnight**, guests walk out onto the river and choose: a thousand copper lanterns, and one by one the gold blooms. Honora stays copper; Kai turns gold. Sloane hesitates, then steps in and turns hers gold. Richard reaches Celestine: "Celie. Beautiful." "It was always beautiful, Richard. It was just half." (LL-65)
- Sloane gives Celestine the unopened envelope. Celestine: "She came to see me. Nineteen days before. She asked me what I wanted. Nobody in your family had ever asked me that." "What did you say?" "The rest is mine." (LL-66)
- Afterwards there is acknowledgment without forgiveness (Honora and Kai), a dry truce (Gideon and Emmett), a slow dance (LL-67), and the family eating pie with their fingers on the back steps (LL-68).

### Arc 7 — Afterlight (GE+1 to GE+9)

- The city talks about the gold on the river without naming anyone (LL-69).
- Every vision goes public by its holder's choice. The menu becomes a free Sunday supper for elders; the couturier hires the grandmother; the song becomes the Crescent Room's Saturday closer; Celestine gives the gold state to the city **under her own name** (LL-70).
- The makers meet to choose next year's visions. Sloane walks past the window and keeps going (LL-71).
- Emmett brings her a tomato plant. The fern has a new leaf (LL-72).
- At Sunday breakfast: Gideon's offer abroad, Nadine's sabbatical, a perfect pancake (LL-73).

**Final release (LL-74).** Celestine opens the envelope. We read two lines in Vivienne's hand: *"I want to stand up in front of everyone we know and say whose light it is."* And: *"I know I'm deciding for you again. That's why this is still in my desk."* Celestine sends one message, to Richard: *Saturday. The Crescent Room. Bring the cane.*

## 09 — Collaborator Character Architecture

### 9.1 Who they are

**CELESTINE RHODES**, 64
- **World:** light art, design and teaching. She works from a studio above an old glassworks and teaches light design at the art school.
- **Want:** to be asked, not arranged; authorship of her own life (which isn't the same as credit); to finish the *yes*.
- **Contradiction:** she teaches a whole city to see, and refuses to be seen by the one family that owes her.
- **Voice:** "Your mother arranged things too."

**MALACHI "KAI" FONTAINE**, 34
- **World:** a self-taught engineer and fabricator from the East Side who builds kinetic light structures, and teaches neighbourhood kids after school.
- **Background:** grew up where privacy protections were thinnest (Bible 02: "wealth disparities in high-end protections").
- **Personality:** exuberant, funny, impatient, brilliant with his hands. He loves Celestine like a second mother.
- **Want:** to come back into the rooms; to have the good his decision did acknowledged without pretending the harm away; to work with Celestine.

### 9.2 Chemistry and history

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ten years ago, aged 24 | Kai was the fabricator chosen, anonymously, for Vivienne's final commission. |
| A month later | When the commission was withdrawn, he went to Celestine's studio on his own and asked to apprentice. **Their collaboration was born from Vivienne's withdrawal, and he chose it himself.** |
| The years together | She has the eye; he has the engineering. They argue like family. |
| Five years ago | They made the **lantern pavilion** for Sloane's first Grand Exchange: about 1,000 hand-blown lanterns, each keyed to a guest's consent. The room was lit by who chose to be seen. It was made for one night, never to be shown again. |

### 9.3 The betrayal (four years ago)

- **The decision.** Kai licensed the pavilion's consent-keyed system to a civic programme for children's hospitals and public housing, without Celestine's consent. She had refused for a year.
- **His reasons were good.** These were the places where people had least control over being captured.
- **The harm.** He demonstrated it on the original rig, whose memory still held the Night's consent keys. **Forty guests' presence at a private Night became known.**

**Why exclusion was justified.** It breached two lines of the code (Bible 03):
- he offered what he didn't solely control;
- his demonstration caused unauthorized exposure.

It was Sloane's first major ruling as leader.

### 9.4 Why reinstatement can now be considered, and why it's still denied

**Why it can be considered:**
- the civic programme works;
- 37 of 40 harmed guests have released him;
- the proposed code allows reinstatement if no harmed party objects;
- Sloane needs him, and is no longer sure her ruling was fair.

**Why it's denied:** Honora objects, and her objection stands (LL-50). Kai attends anyway, by a one-night **Threshold** that Honora chooses not to oppose (LL-52).

### 9.5 Why Sloane needs both, and why no one else can replace them

- The centerpiece *is* Celestine's unrealized *yes*.
- It must be built on the original lantern system, which is **co-owned**: neither can ethically use it without the other's consent.
- Celestine has the eye; Kai has the engineering.
- A substitute maker would mean using one co-owner's work without the other's consent. That's the very violation the season is about.

### 9.6 How the reunion evolves

| Step | What happens | Release |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kai nominates Celestine's vision without standing. | LL-05 |
| 2 | Celestine returns Sloane's invitation unopened. | LL-10 |
| 3 | Kai's amends continue: 37 of 40. | LL-19 |
| 4 | Kai brings Celestine to the ward, and she agrees to hear him without Sloane. | LL-38 |
| 5 | Celestine walks out over Sloane's unsealing request. | LL-39 |
| 6 | Asked, not arranged, Celestine returns on her own terms and requests a Threshold. | LL-51 |
| 7 | They work, fight, laugh and nearly quit. It works. | LL-55 |
| 8 | Under pressure, they make a decision *together*: they erase the old rig's record of the first Night. | LL-64 |
| 9 | They unveil the *yes*. | LL-65 |
| 10 | The result is a working peace and a renewed love, with the injury acknowledged. Not full reconciliation. | LL-65, LL-70 |

### 9.7 The rhyme, and how it avoids feeling too neat

Kai did to Celestine what Vivienne did: he gave a shared creation to the public good without consent.

To keep this from feeling engineered:
1. **The acts differ in kind.**

   | | Vivienne | Kai |
   |---|---|---|
   | What she or he gave | The design itself, a civic standard | A license, plus a demonstration that exposed people |
   | Credit | Erased Celestine's authorship | Didn't erase it |
   | People exposed | None | Forty |

2. **Characters notice and argue about it** (LL-48: "I punished him for her." "You didn't know." "That's not a reason.").
3. **The rhyme breaks at the climax.** Celestine and Kai make the next decision *together* (LL-64).

**Founder decision required:** the Ember backstory touches world history (FD-05, §26).

## 10 — Vivienne's Final Exchange: Proposed Truth

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Full detail, including the studio conversation and the invitation's full text, is in the writers'-room truth document (§2–§6).

**What she did:**

| Element | What happened | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Professional face** | Through The Exchange, Vivienne set up an anonymous sponsored commission. It was Celestine's first major public work in decades, with a young fabricator Vivienne had scouted (Kai). She sealed the beneficiary, and was never named. It was restitution disguised as an opportunity. | About six weeks before her death |
| **The invitation she chose not to send** | Addressed *C. R.* She meant to stand up at that year's Grand Exchange and say whose light the Ember was. She sealed it and kept it in her desk, because sending it would decide for Celestine, again, how her story was told. | About four weeks before |
| **The undisclosed private interaction** | She went alone, at night, to Celestine's studio, after 21 years. They fought, laughed, and talked until dawn. Celestine: "Every time you love someone, you arrange something for them. Stop arranging me." She asked Vivienne to withdraw the commission and come back in a year with nothing in her hands. Vivienne agreed. | Twenty days before |
| **The deliberate withdrawal** | She withdrew the commission herself: the only exchange she ever withdrew. | Nineteen days before |
| **Death** | A ruptured brain aneurysm, on a Sunday morning, in the family kitchen, with Richard there. **Unrelated to the exchange.** "Linked" survives only as family belief, because grief fused two events nineteen days apart. | — |

**Why it's emotionally surprising.** Vivienne's last act wasn't heroic sacrifice or a scandal. It was **her first real attempt to stop deciding for someone she loved.** Her daughter makes the same choice in Season 1 without knowing her mother made it first.

**Why it's causally coherent.**
- The commission's withdrawal is what brought Kai to Celestine (§09).
- The Ember explains Celestine's grievance, the "second state" lecture, the copper glow, and the rhyme with Kai.
- The sealed beneficiary explains why Sloane's request reaches Celestine.

**Why it's worth several seasons.**
- S1 reveals the commission, the Ember, and that the visit happened.
- S2 reveals what was said, the promise to come back in a year, and what Celestine does with the invitation.
- S3 settles whether the Ember's whole history becomes public, and what that does to the Lockwood name and to the world's founding story.

**Guardrails kept.**
- No murder.
- No villain father.
- No secret child.
- No missing file. The ledger entry and envelope are in plain sight, and the question is who has the right to open them.
- Vivienne stays a complete woman: brilliant, generous to the point of control, funny, capable of changing.
- Sloane isn't entitled to the answer because she's the daughter.

**Why the privacy rules matter:**
- Royce's inference is lawful, but announcing it is a social violation he gets away with.
- Richard broke a confidence, which is shameful for a former practitioner.
- Marcus repeated family-table talk, which breaks an unspoken norm.

- Sloane's own letter and Kai's visits to the Salon point at Kai (LL-24, LL-27).

**Consequences:**
- Nadine is wounded.
- Kai is wronged.
- The makers waver.
- Celestine uses it against Sloane.
- Royce gains.
- Richard is ashamed.

**Sloane's answer:** a consented, public disclosure on her own terms (LL-63).

### 16.2 Why 74 releases, and not the draft's 80 or WORD OF MOUTH's 44

- **The story determined the count.** Each release carries one distinct beat or experience. When a draft release didn't, it was merged.
- **About a minute each, as the founder asked.** At around one minute per release, a season of this shape takes 70–80 releases to tell. WORD OF MOUTH's 44 releases averaged 3:19, and 32 of them were STORY releases of 3–6 minutes. That length suits a different format.
- **LIFE plus WORLD is 31% of releases, and 51% with HYBRID included,** against WORD OF MOUTH's 23%. That is the "meaningful portion" Sprint 4 §11 asks for.
- **The runtime of about 101 minutes** is 31% shorter than WORD OF MOUTH's 146 minutes.

## 20 — The Living Legacy: Full Experience Design

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.**

### 20.1 Principle

> **"The night is private. The legacy is public, if they say so."**

### 20.2 What is exchanged

| Party | Gives |
|---|---|
| The holder | Trust, and a vision they could never realize alone |
| The maker | Skill and labour |
| The Exchange | Materials (as a sponsored case) and a private place to make |

**No money passes between holder and maker. The consideration is the work.** That keeps it true to the property's name: it is an exchange, not a prize.

### 20.3 The rules

1. **Nobody applies.** A vision is nominated by someone who loves the person who carries it. Nobody competes, pitches or is judged in public.
2. **The holder must consent** to being nominated, and can decline.
3. **Pairing.** This year, Sloane pairs each vision with an unlikely maker. From next year, this year's makers do the choosing and pairing (Sloane's exit, designed into the tradition).
4. **The making is private,** under Exchange terms: consented and unrecorded.
5. **Unveiling** happens at the Grand Exchange, under Private Presence.
6. **Afterwards, each holder decides** whether the work goes into the world, in what form, and under whose name. Anything shown publicly follows **consented display with revocation**, Emmett's precedent (LL-53).
7. **Standing.** Nominators need standing in The Exchange. Kai has none, so his nomination is covered by the Threshold (LL-51, LL-52), for this one purpose only.

### 20.4 Season 1 visions

| Vision | Holder (nominator) | Maker | What's made | How guests experience it | What continues |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ezekiel's Supper** | Clementine Hollis, 80 (great-niece Dara) | Femi Adeyemi, 29 | Her late father's unwritten dining-car menu | Served to 200 guests, his name on every card (LL-61) | Femi's free Sunday supper for elders (LL-70) |
| **The 1981 Gown** | Inez Carter, 70 (granddaughter Talia) | Rashid Bello, 31 | The gown she drew "for being looked at" | Worn by Talia all night; it opens like a bell on the dance floor (LL-58, LL-62) | Rashid hires Inez (LL-70) |
| **Noelle's Song** | Noelle Baptiste, 16 (her grandmother) | Clarence Moody, 74, and the Crescent Room Orchestra | A big-band arrangement of a song she's written since she was twelve | The floor fills (LL-62) | The Crescent Room's Saturday closer (LL-70) |
| **The Yes** (centerpiece) | Celestine Rhodes, 64 (Kai, by Threshold) | Celestine and Kai | The Ember's lost gold state: a river of a thousand lanterns, each revocable | The midnight walk: each guest chooses (LL-65) | Given to the city under her own name (LL-70) |

### 20.5 Spectacle design

The River Hall night has three movements:

| Time | Movement | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 p.m. | **Taste:** the supper | Memory |
| 10:00 p.m. | **Sound:** the song | Joy |
| Midnight | **Light:** the *yes* | Choice |

The gown is the through-line, worn all night.

**The image the season is built toward:** guests walking out of a private Night onto a river of copper lanterns, and turning them gold one by one by choice. The city across the water sees only those who chose to be seen.

### 20.6 Why it's believable in a privacy-first world

- The making is private.
- The unveiling is private.
- Going public is the holder's choice.
- Display is revocable.
- Copper is honoured as much as gold (Honora, LL-65).

**It's the first thing an Exchange Night has ever sent out into the world, and it does so only by consent.**

### 20.7 What can go wrong (Season 2 material)

- The makers choose a vision Sloane would have refused.
- A holder declines publication.
- Royce launches a public, vote-based imitation at the Salon.
- A dispute arises over credit.

## 21 — Grand Exchange Finale

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Releases LL-56 to LL-68, plus the Afterlight releases LL-69 to LL-74.

**Why the evening is extraordinary.**
- **A setting:** a restored ferry terminal on the river.
