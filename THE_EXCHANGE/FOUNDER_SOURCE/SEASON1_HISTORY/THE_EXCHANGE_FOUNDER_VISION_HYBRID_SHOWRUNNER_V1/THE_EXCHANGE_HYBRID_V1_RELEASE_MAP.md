**Length.**
- Average release: 1:22.
- STORY releases: mostly 1:15–2:00. Climactic ones run to 3:30 (LL-65).
- LIFE and WORLD releases: 0:40–1:15.

**Production complexity:** 39 LOW, 22 MED, 8 HIGH, 5 VERY HIGH.
- The five VERY HIGH releases are LL-04 (the opening party), LL-23 (the Salon), LL-60 (arrivals), LL-62 (the dance) and LL-65 (the *yes*).

### 16.2 Why 74 releases, and not the draft's 80 or WORD OF MOUTH's 44

- **The story determined the count.** Each release carries one distinct beat or experience. When a draft release didn't, it was merged.
- **About a minute each, as the founder asked.** At around one minute per release, a season of this shape takes 70–80 releases to tell. WORD OF MOUTH's 44 releases averaged 3:19, and 32 of them were STORY releases of 3–6 minutes. That length suits a different format.
- **LIFE plus WORLD is 31% of releases, and 51% with HYBRID included,** against WORD OF MOUTH's 23%. That is the "meaningful portion" Sprint 4 §11 asks for.
- **The runtime of about 101 minutes** is 31% shorter than WORD OF MOUTH's 146 minutes.

### 16.3 How to read each entry

The ID is followed by the 13 sprint fields, in the template's order.

**Story time** is given in SETTING as days before or after Grand Exchange day ("GE−45"). On Grand Exchange day it's given as clock time.

**Cross-references** use LL numbers.

**Arc names are internal.** Public releases carry titles, not numbers.

### Arc 1 · The Host (11 releases, 14:05)

#### LL-01 · Dusk

- **ID:** LL-01
- **INTERNAL ARC:** 1 · The Host
- **CONTENT CATEGORY:** WORLD
- **WORKING TITLE:** Dusk
- **DURATION:** 0:45
- **FEATURED CHARACTERS:** City residents; a woman on a balcony; her neighbour (no principals)
- **SETTING:** A street of brownstones and glass towers at dusk. GE−92 (story time).
- **WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS:** The city dresses for the night: a tailor locks up, a teenager practises a dance step at a bus stop, a grandmother hangs a jewel-green dress on a fire escape to air. On a balcony, a woman dances alone in her kitchen. Across the street, a neighbour lifts a phone toward her. The lens glows copper: the Ember, the city's everyday "no." The neighbour lowers it, embarrassed. She raises a glass to him and keeps dancing. No dialogue, music only.
- **CHARACTER PURPOSE:** Introduces the world before introducing anyone in it.
- **STORY OR EXPERIENTIAL VALUE:** The world's founding grammar (being seen is not being captured) is shown, not explained. The copper glow is the season's most rewatchable image: in LL-48 it is revealed as Celestine and Vivienne's design.
- **CONTINUITY DEPENDENCIES:** Defines the Ember's look (copper glow) for the whole season.
- **ENDING / TRANSITION:** The camera rises over rooftops toward a lit ballroom: Halcyon House.
- **PRODUCTION COMPLEXITY:** MED (exteriors, several extras, a light effect, music)
