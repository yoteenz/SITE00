> **NONCANONICAL. PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Every event, name, date and line in this document is a development proposal. It records the underlying reality the writers would need, so that every clue is fair and every reveal is planned. It changes no canon, no approval record and no repository.

**Purpose.** Sprint 4 §15 asks for a truth document that proves the story has a coherent underlying reality. ## 2. The studio visit: what actually happened (Y−10, 20 days before Vivienne's death)

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED. Writers know this. The audience does not learn it in Season 1. Sloane does not learn it in Season 1.**

1. **Vivienne arrives at about 10 p.m.** with nothing but the commission's terms in her coat. Celestine lets her in without a word and makes tea neither drinks.
2. **They fight.** It's about the garage, the newspaper, and twenty-one years. Celestine: "You didn't give the world a door. You gave it half a sentence and signed my name to nothing."
3. **Then they laugh,** about the record they danced to the night the gold first worked. Vivienne still knows the words; Celestine doesn't.
4. **They talk until dawn:** children, Richard, Celestine's students, getting older.
5. **Celestine names the pattern:** "Every time you love someone, you arrange something for them. A commission. A credit. A room. Stop arranging me."
6. **What Celestine actually wants:**
   - not credit;
   - to be asked;
   - for the *yes* to exist one day on her own terms.
7. **She asks Vivienne** to withdraw the commission and come back in a year with nothing in her hands: no commission, no invitation, no credit, just herself.
   - Vivienne: "Not even cake?" Celestine: "Not even cake."
8. **Vivienne agrees.** She says one thing she has never said to anyone: "I think I've been useful to people instead of being with them, my whole life."
9. **She doesn't mention the invitation in her desk.** Celestine never knows it exists until Sloane hands it to her (LL-66).
10. **Celestine doesn't know, and never will learn in Season 1,** whether Vivienne would have come back in a year. Vivienne died nineteen days later. Celestine has carried an unfinished year for a decade.

## 3. The invitation: full text (sealed about 4 weeks before Vivienne's death)

**PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED.** Handwritten on Exchange invitation stock. The audience sees only the two lines marked ▶ (LL-74).

> C. —
>
> The Grand Exchange is the second Saturday in October. Come.
>
> ▶ I want to stand up in front of everyone we know and say whose light it is.
>
> I've written this four times. Every version decides something for you: where, and in front of whom, and in what words. I keep telling myself it's a gift. It's a gift the way the garage was a gift.
>
> ▶ I know I'm deciding for you again. That's why this is still in my desk.
>
> If you ever want me to stop deciding, tell me what you want instead. I'll come.
>
> — V.

**What the full text reveals to the writers.** Vivienne understood her own pattern *before* the studio visit. She went to the studio instead of sending this. The visit was her first attempt not to arrange. **Her withdrawal was an act of growth, not failure:** she chose, for once, not to decide for someone she loved.

## 4. Why Vivienne withdrew (Sloane's unanswered question)

- **The factual reason:** Celestine asked her to.
- **The deeper reason:** Vivienne saw her lifelong habit, love expressed as arrangement, and chose to try to stop.
- **Death:** unrelated to the withdrawal.
- **What Sloane gets in Season 1:** she makes the same choice without knowing her mother made it first. She stops arranging Celestine (LL-42), gives the envelope back unopened (LL-66), and steps back from deciding (LL-63). **The rhyme is the emotional truth of the season.** The writers hold it; Sloane doesn't.

## 5. Vivienne's death

- A ruptured brain aneurysm, at 55, on a Sunday morning, in the family kitchen. Sudden. Richard was there.
- **Not linked causally** to the exchange, the withdrawal or anyone's conduct.
- The bible's language, "death linked to a final exchange," survives only as **family belief**. The two events were nineteen days apart, and grief fused them. This is a founder decision: the bible lists cause of death as UNRESOLVED.
- No murder, no culpable father, no secret child, no missing file.

## 6. The Ember: world facts (proposed)

- The Ember is the region's civic consent signal: a soft **copper** glow that any device shows when capture is declined. It has been universal for about thirty years. Its design is unattributed, as is common for civic standards.
- The original design had a second state, a **gold bloom** meaning *I choose to be seen*. It was never adopted. Lighting designers know a gold state was once proposed; nobody outside a few people knows by whom.
- **People who know the Ember's true authorship at the start of Season 1:** Celestine and Richard. Vivienne's own notebooks in the family archive mention it, but nobody has read them closely.
- **Who learns it in Season 1:** Sloane (LL-48), and Nadine (after LL-54, offscreen; she asks "Who's Celestine?" and Sloane says "Later"). The public does not learn the "no" half's authorship. Celestine gives the city the gold state under her own name (LL-70), without telling its history.
