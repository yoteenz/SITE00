# Sloane — selective narration and freeze-frame grammar

**Classification:** PROVISIONAL CONCEPT / FOUNDER-SUPPORTED DIRECTION  
**Not** a mandatory per-scene or per-episode rule.

## Selective first-person narration

Signature **proposed** device: audience occasionally experiences the world through Sloane's **private** perspective.

She may narrate introductions to: important characters, Exchange Nights, social customs, guest histories, relationships, unusual dynamics, her contradictions, select developments.

### Voice qualities

Intelligent, sophisticated, charming, witty, observant, glamorous, occasionally mischievous, emotionally human.

### Avoid

Constant narration; generic exposition; overexplaining mythology; excessive jokes; sitcom commentary; forced punchlines; juvenile slang; narration that repeats what the camera already shows.

**Goal:** occasional access to **how it feels inside her mind** — not Sloane explaining the entire series.

## Subjective characterization (not omniscient)

Narration is **not** reliably authoritative. It reveals perception. Sloane may be insightful, affectionate, biased, incorrect, defensive, playfully judgmental, guarded, or oblivious to her own feelings.

Discrepancy between Sloane's voice and objective events is **intentional** storytelling space — not accidental inconsistency.

### Romantic vulnerability (proposed arc — partner UNKNOWN)

Early VO about a potential interest may be confident, playful, dismissive, or teasing. As feelings deepen, observations may become less certain, more revealing, hesitant, less frequent, emotionally conflicted. The woman who reads others well may struggle with her own feelings — **silence** may carry meaning.

**Do not** introduce romantic partner identity without founder approval.

## Editorial freeze-frame introductions (proposed grammar)

For **selected** pivotal characters only:

1. Character enters or performs a revealing action.  
2. Motion briefly freezes at a composed moment.  
3. Elegant on-screen title: character name.  
4. Concise editorial descriptor.  
5. Sloane VO: witty or perceptive introduction.  
6. Motion resumes naturally.  
7. Immediate behavior may reinforce, complicate, or contradict her commentary.  

Sophisticated and visually intentional — avoid cartoonish effects or intrusive graphics. Not for every guest. Aids recognition and characterization without formula overload.

## Gideon — introductory narration tone (proposed)

Not a list of combat credentials first. Sloane's affectionate, mildly exasperated lens: longtime family intimate, protector, confidant, liaison, uncle-like figure, can interrupt without ceremony, unsolicited advice, genuinely understands her.

Formidable professional history emerges **gradually**. To outsiders he may be internationally respected; to Sloane he is simply **Gideon**. Warmth, humor, loyalty, depth — not stereotypical intimidating bodyguard.

## When not to narrate

Preserve **silence** when performance carries the moment: grief, intimacy, vulnerability, difficult family revelations, confrontations, meaningful personal decisions, moments requiring independent audience interpretation.

Narration is an artistic tool — use only when it adds narrative, emotional, comedic, or character value.
