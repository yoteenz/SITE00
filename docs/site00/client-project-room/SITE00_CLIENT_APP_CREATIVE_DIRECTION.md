## What the client app should feel like

The app should be a private room in a quiet, bright building. The client walks in and the work is on the wall. Someone has left a short note saying where things stand and the one thing that needs them.

It should feel:

- **quiet** and **light** — mostly white and air;
- **precise** — hairline rules, uppercase, exact words;
- **premium** and **editorial** — the work is presented like a published spread, not a list of tickets;
- **human** — sentences, not status codes;
- **confident** — the room already knows the answers.

It belongs to **SITE 00**: Martian Mono host type, red only where something matters, and the project's own colour only in the ◆ marker and the work itself.

It must never feel like a task manager, a developer dashboard, a production console, an accounting app or a wall of cards. The production workspace is dense and instrumented. The client room is its calm mirror: the same truth with the instruments removed.

## What HOME should feel like

A front desk with a note on it. One sentence is the **current moment**:

> WAITING ON YOU — IDENTITY DIRECTION REVIEW. WHEN YOU APPROVE, WE MOVE INTO FAMILY 01.

Below that sit the few things that **need you**, each with one button. Then **what comes next**, with an honest estimate of what it will require and cost. Then a short line of what you recently approved, and three human updates.

When nothing needs the client, HOME says so plainly:

> NOTHING NEEDS YOU — WE'RE IN PRODUCTION ON FAMILY 02.

Silence is a feature.

## What PROJECT should feel like

The full map, folded so it can be read on a phone. The project summary is one paragraph of facts: scope, phase, progress, price and production expenses.

The journey reads **COMPLETED · CURRENT · NEXT · LATER**. Families read like chapters, each with a name, a purpose, a status in plain words, its approval, and an estimated or actual cost. The timeline is a human sentence per step, never a Gantt chart. Tapping a family opens a sheet; nothing navigates away.

## What a REVIEW should feel like

A private viewing. The authority under review fills the screen, and chrome recedes to a thin top line and the two decisions at the bottom. Context sits under the work and is short:

- family name and purpose;
- what changed;
- the version;
- what this family cost.

Key children and responsive views sit in a calm horizontal sequence, not a grid. The REVIEWS list groups work as **NEEDS YOU / REVISION IN PROGRESS / APPROVED**, the way a gallery groups rooms.

## What APPROVAL should feel like

**♥ APPROVE** reads as "yes, this feels right". It is a full-width labelled button, with the heart as a small mark beside the word. It is not a like, a swipe or a social gesture.

Tapping it settles the work. A brief APPROVED state with a short UNDO window replaces the controls, and the room moves forward. The client is not asked to explain an approval; an optional ADD A NOTE is there for anyone who wants it. Only final or contractual gates ask for an explicit confirmation.

Approval never shows a price or a payment button. If the next family needs funding, that appears afterwards, as the next step.

## What REVISION should feel like

**× NEEDS REVISION** reads as "this needs work — here's why". Tapping it does not submit anything. The page opens a space to talk:

> WHAT ISN'T WORKING FOR YOU?

There is a large field and a row of optional plain-language chips (VISUAL DIRECTION · LAYOUT · COPY · COLOR · IMAGERY · INTERACTION · DOESN'T FEEL LIKE MY BRAND · OTHER), and at most one gentle follow-up: WHAT SHOULD WE KEEP?

SEND REVISION REQUEST stays quiet until there is something meaningful to send. The client never needs design vocabulary. Drafts are never lost.

When the new version arrives, the review remembers: **what you said · what changed · the new version**.

## What COST TRANSPARENCY should feel like

A clear receipt held by someone you trust. It is not an invoice dashboard. Amounts are shown in dollars first, with credits second ("ABOUT 6,000–8,000 CREDITS"). Estimates always say ESTIMATED and give a range; completed families say ACTUAL.

The cost center answers four questions in one glance:

- How much has been spent?
- What is the next family likely to cost?
- What is still deferred?
- Who is paying?

If the project is client-funded, PRODUCTION CREDITS shows the provider, its status and the balance this project has used. It never shows a key.

## What INBOX should feel like

A correspondence drawer for people. It holds threads with the founder about things that are not a review: a question, a decision, an upload request, a clarification. A request inside a thread can be answered in place.

Project status never requires a message, and agents never speak here.

## What LIBRARY should feel like

A clean archive the client owns, arranged as identity, approved work, deliverables, references, their uploads and final files. Every file carries a name, a version and a date, and downloads in one tap. It never shows the internal vault, drafts or production plates.

## How the client stays informed without being overwhelmed

- **One pulse.** The same derived state drives HOME, notifications and the founder HUB, so the client never sees two versions of the truth.
- **Notifications only when something needs them or changed for them.** That means NEEDS YOU, REVISION READY, APPROVAL RECEIVED, FILE READY, FUNDING REQUIRED and important changes. They never cover asset mounts, QA passes or retries.
- **Curated updates.** Activity is translated into human sentences, three at a time.

## How SITE 00 builds trust

The room answers questions before they are asked:

- why something is waiting;
- who needs to act;
- what changed;
- what a revision means;
- what was spent and what remains;
- what happens after approval.

Estimates are honest ranges learned from real ledgers, and nothing is invented: no fake metrics, no fixture numbers. Approvals are permanent history; a reversal is a new event, never a rewrite.

## How the system reduces founder labor

The founder stops writing "where we are / what I need / what's next / what it cost / what you approved / what I'm working on" emails. All six are derived from project state. The founder's controls become a few deliberate acts:

- **SEND TO CLIENT**
- **APPROVE ESTIMATE FOR VISIBILITY**
- **CLASSIFY A SCOPE CHANGE**
- **PUBLISH TO LIBRARY**

Every client action lands in the production INBOX client lens. If a client still has to ask any of the routine questions, the room has failed and the gap is a bug.
