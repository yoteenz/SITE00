## Rules for every voice

- Write people, not systems. A line explains the world only if a person in the scene would say it.
- Established lines are anchors, not a quota. Use each where the master places it; never repeat one to prove personality.
- Black American and queer creative vocabulary belongs to specific people in specific rooms. It is never a quota, a seasoning or a joke.
- Characters may interrupt, trail off and be wrong. Variation is protected: no character speaks in a single register all season.
- Silence is dialogue. Grief, intimacy, confrontation and decisive choices default to performance, not narration.
- Sloane's voice-over is selective, subjective and may be wrong. It never repeats what the camera shows.

The screenplay validator reads this registry: an unregistered speaker is a hard conflict unless the screenplay declares the character in `PRODUCTION.characters` (then a warning), and overused signature lines are flagged.

## Gideon Shaw (`GIDEON`, 44)

**Voice identity.** Low, unhurried, economical. Warmth arrives as dry understatement. Never raises his voice; does not need to.

**Linguistic personality**

- **vocabulary:** Plain words, precise nouns, no jargon or tactical talk in social rooms.
- **phrasing:** Statements, not threats. Corrections delivered as facts ("Your hair's perfect. Stop touching it.").
- **humor:** Deadpan; acts of care disguised as logistics (removing the cake plate).
